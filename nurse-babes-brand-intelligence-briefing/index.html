Executive Summary

Nurse Babes is a mature DTC footwear brand for nurses and healthcare professionals with 565,000+ customers, an established $117 AOV, and an NPS of 28.77. The brand is in mid-transition: founded as a product brand ("affordable orthopedic-grade comfort for nurses") and now quietly evolving toward an identity brand ("Alo for nurses — help nurses show up strong"). Visible signals of the pivot include a spring 2025 logo refresh, an apparel content track, and explicit brand-doc language declaring "we don't sell shoes, we sell a way of being."

The hero product is the Ortho Pro 1.0, a lightweight slip-resistant supportive sneaker positioned in the affordable-comfort gap between $50 disposables and $150–$200 premium tier (Hoka, Clove, Brooks). The dominant offer is Buy 2 Get 1 Free, which has driven a +27% AOV lift over 17 months but is showing fatigue alongside a 2.24→1.93 Facebook ROAS decline as spend scales.

Today (May 14, 2026) the brand launched Comfort Club, a loyalty/membership program with zero existing creative — the direct lever for the brand's #1 KPI priority of lifting repeat-purchase rate from ~10%.

Meta Compliance Lock

HARD RULE for all paid ads: Meta rejects health/medical claims. Customer testimonials (third-party voice) are exempt but in-house copy is not. Every Step 7 brief includes a compliance review.

Avoid (in paid ad copy)

  • "Pain-free" / "pain free"
  • "Eliminates pain" / "relief from pain"
  • "Cures" / "treats" / "heals" / "fixes"
  • "Medical-grade" / "therapeutic"
  • "Clinically proven" / "FDA-approved"
  • "Plantar fasciitis cure"
  • "Arthritis relief"
  • "Knee/back pain solution"
  • Functional ortho/orthopedic claims

Use Instead

  • "All-day comfort"
  • "Supportive" / "support"
  • "Designed for long shifts"
  • "Easy on your feet"
  • "Built for 12-hour days"
  • "Cloud-like comfort"
  • "Feels like nothing"
  • "Forget you have shoes on"
  • "Show up strong"
Exempt zone: Customer testimonial quotes (Trustpilot, surveys, UGC) remain exempt from this rule because they're third-party voice attestations. The user said "MY FEET NEVER HURT ANYMORE" — we can show that quote. We can't have brand-written copy say "feet won't hurt anymore."

Post-Pipeline Ad Diagnostic (Adrienne's Client Question, May 14)

Two specific ads surfaced by the client after the main pipeline. Both analyzed below with action plans.

Ad #1 — AC148 (May 7, 2026): Engagement Good, CPA Bad

Context: 30-second Nurse Week reaction video. $200 brief budget. Format: 15 seconds silent greenscreen reaction watching another reel + 15 seconds soft poetic script delivering Buy 2 Get 1 Free Nurse Week offer. Media buyer killed it on CPA basis despite strong engagement.

Diagnostic — Why engagement was good but spend was bad

  1. Format ≠ Funnel. Reaction videos optimize for engagement signals (saves, shares, comments). They're built to entertain, not convert. They're MOF/retention assets, not TOF acquisition.
  2. Hook lives at second 15, not second 3. Meta acquisition needs the offer + value-prop in the first 3 seconds. By second 15, the cold viewer has already swiped on a TOF placement.
  3. "For one week a year" kills evergreen. Nurse-Week-windowed ad. Meta needs 7-10 days to optimize spend, but the offer was a 7-day window. The algorithm couldn't catch up before the message went stale.
  4. Soft poetic delivery buries the BOGO mechanic. The "And one, completely on us" line is the offer — but it's delivered as the payoff, not the hook. A nurse scrolling cold doesn't get to the payoff.
  5. BOGO fatigue meets soft framing. Per Step 4, B2G1 is fatigued at 21 months. Pairing it with even softer delivery weakens it further.

Verdict & Re-Deploy

The media buyer made the right call on CPA grounds. But the format isn't broken — the funnel placement was wrong. Don't kill the asset. Re-deploy as MOF/retention against:

  • 50%+ video viewers from acquisition creative (warm audience)
  • Cart abandoners (in-purchase consideration)
  • Comfort Club non-members (loyalty conversion)

In those contexts, the reaction format does what it's actually good at: emotional reinforcement after offer awareness already exists. Run it during Nurse Week to warm audiences — not as the cold-acquisition workhorse.
