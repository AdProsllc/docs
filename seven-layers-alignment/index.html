VISUAL STUDY · 06 TREATMENTS

The Seven Layers
of Alignment

A study of nested selves — from the body outward to the infinite. Six visual treatments, each carrying a different argument about how alignment works and where intervention happens.

FIG. 01 / STRATIFIED MODEL

The Stratosphere

Geological · Vertical · Gross to subtle

A cross-section of self. The body is the bedrock; the digital is the ionosphere. Each layer rests on the one beneath. Misalignment is geological — slow, structural, and felt long before it's seen.

FIG. 01

What this sells

That alignment is foundational. A misaligned body cannot support an aligned spirit. A misaligned spirit cannot project coherently into the digital. The stack metaphor makes intervention obvious: start where the crack is, not where the symptom is.

When to use it

Best when arguing for sequence — that someone can't skip layers. Echoes Dilts' logical levels and the OSI model. Reads top-down or bottom-up; both directions tell a story.

FIG. 02 / RADIAL MODEL

Ripples Outward

Concentric · Radial · Self at center

Drop a pebble in water. The body is the pebble; each layer is the next wave. The digital is the ripple still moving long after you can no longer see it.

FIG. 02

What this sells

That alignment propagates. What happens at the Physical layer doesn't stay there — it becomes Emotional, Psychological, and eventually shows up Digitally. The ripple implies the source is small and the effect is vast.

When to use it

Best when arguing that inner work has external consequences. Echoes Bronfenbrenner's ecological systems and Hierocles' Circles of Concern. Strong for showing why personal practice matters at scale.

FIG. 03 / VISIBILITY MODEL

The Iceberg

Visible / Hidden · What shows · What runs

Most of what determines you sits beneath the waterline. The Physical, Relational, Environmental, and Digital are visible. The Emotional, Psychological, and Spiritual are not — and yet they steer everything above.

FIG. 03

What this sells

That what's online is downstream of what's interior. You can't fix your digital presence by editing your digital presence. The fix lives below the surface. The iceberg makes this visceral.

When to use it

Best when arguing against surface-level fixes. Especially powerful for a digital-native audience that lives at the top and forgets the bottom exists. Echoes the classic Hemingway iceberg theory.

FIG. 04 / CIRCULATORY MODEL

Toroidal Flow

Circulation · Energy · Alignment as flow

Energy rises from Physical, through the inner three, out through the outer three, into the Digital — and loops back. Alignment is circulation. Misalignment is blockage.

FIG. 04

What this sells

That alignment is dynamic, not static. The layers aren't shelves you check off — they're a system that has to keep moving. When one layer is blocked, the whole loop suffers. Closest in spirit to chakras and the kosha onion.

When to use it

Best when your audience is somatically literate — open to language like "energy" and "flow." The most spiritually-coded of the four. Could feel powerful or could feel woo, depending on the audience.

FIG. + / EXPERIMENTAL TREATMENTS

Further Sketches

Experimental · Less polished · More provocative

Six additional treatments worth considering. Some are stronger metaphors; some are stronger diagrams. Each prioritizes a different aspect of the model.

SKETCH A

The Matryoshka

Nested dolls · each contains the next

A nesting doll metaphor. Each layer literally contains the one beneath. Sells the idea that the inner self is preserved inside every outer expression — and that no layer is left behind.

SKETCH B

The Density Spectrum

Gross to subtle · matter to information
Physical
DENSE
Emotional
Psychological
Spiritual
Relational
Environmental
Digital
SUBTLE

A logarithmic gradient from dense (physical) to subtle (digital). Borrowed from the Pancha Koshas. Makes the case that the Digital isn't less real — just less dense. Most defensible philosophically.

SKETCH C

The Constellation

Network · non-hierarchical · interdependent

Drops the hierarchy. Treats the 7 layers as nodes in a constellation, each pulling on the others. Useful when arguing that no layer is more important — alignment is the geometry between them.

SKETCH D

The Climbing Ladder

Sequential · progressive · maturity arc

Strictly ordered. Each layer is a rung; you can't skip. Borrows from Maslow and Dilts. Best when teaching the model as a developmental sequence — where are you currently spending most of your attention?

SKETCH E

The Scale of Being

Editorial · printable · reference card

A clean, printable reference. No metaphor, no embellishment — just the 7 layers labeled and ordered with subtitles. Strongest format for a one-pager, a wall print, or the back of a card.

SKETCH F

The Solar System

Orbital · gravitational · self at center

Body is the sun. Each layer orbits at greater radius. Digital is the outer planet — distant, vast, but still gravitationally bound to the core. Implies the inner self exerts pull on the outer layers, not the reverse.
