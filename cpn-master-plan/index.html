01 THE MODEL

The Model on One Page

The One-Page Companion — forward this, not the doc

The model. CPN runs creator programs end-to-end for ecommerce brands. Clients pay $5K/month or 5% of Meta-attributed revenue (7-day click / 1-day view), whichever applies. The unit is the video, planned at $5K/month attributed. Funnel: 95% recording · 20% win · 4% breakthrough.

The constraint. Partner-sourced lead flow: 2 qualified leads/week today vs. 6.7 needed. 3 of 30+ partners are active. Activation — not a new channel — closes the gap.

The three numbers. Close rate (30%) · $/video ($5K) · win rate (20%). Everything else is decoration.

Next three actions. 1) Interview the 3 active partners this week (Nehal). 2) Ship the creator agreement — it gates the first close (Nehal). 3) Stand up brief system v1 from the existing Creative Matrix (Omar seat).

02 THE BINDING CONSTRAINT

The Demand Engine

Partner activation is the whole game. 3 active partners produce 2 qualified leads/week (~0.67 each). The target needs 6.7/week at a 30% close rate for 2 closes/week. Ten active partners at the same per-partner yield gets there — from a pool of 30+ already known, with a 10% lifetime referral already on the table. No new channel required.

Mechanism, not just math: partner programs die of neglect, not terms. The playbook is an activation cadence — partner one-pager, 15-minute certification call, monthly leaderboard touch, concierge treatment on the first referral. Before scaling terms to 27 strangers, interview the 3 actives and clone whatever makes them send leads. New activations get 10% year-one with renewal-on-performance; lifetime stays grandfathered for the proven 3 — lifetime-on-both-streams to unproven partners compounds into a permanent tax on your best future clients.

The 100/100/100 pilot is the close accelerator — its entire job is the close-rate number, the single most sensitive input in the model. Best-source vs. most-time mismatch stands corrected in this plan: partners get the hours; content keeps compounding in the background.

Counter-argument (kept honest) The pool may be unengaged because the offer doesn't sell to partners, not because nobody called. If 6 weeks of activation yields under 4 leads/week, the fix is repositioning the offer — discovered around day 40, per the Grind scenario.
03 CHURN PREVENTION

The Deal & Contract Layer

The contract is churn-prevention infrastructure. Base rates say performance agencies die commercial deaths: attribution disputes and results-expectation gaps kill more of them than operations ever do. Both are prevented on paper, before the first close.

The deal: $5K/month retainer OR 5% of attributed revenue — crossover at $100K/month attributed (~20 live videos). Attribution truth is contractual: Meta default, 7-day click / 1-day view, billed from a standard report the client sees every week in exactly the terms the invoice uses. The divergence protocol — what happens when the client's dashboard disagrees — is pre-agreed at signature, not negotiated mid-dispute. The CFO objection ("you bill on your own ruler, and view-through inflates it") is answered in the sale: the LTV/new-customer math is run with the client's own margin before signature, and month-1 expectations include the ramp curve and a testing-budget line ($200–500 learn spend per video), so blended ROAS below 1.5x in month one is a stated plan, not a surprise.

04 SEQUENCED, NOT SOLVED

The Supply Engine

Supply is solvable — but it must 10x within a month of demand unlocking. Each close pulls ~54 creators; 2 closes/week means ~108 activations/week at steady state against ~10/week today. Onboarding is automated (4 days product-to-video, 95% recording, 10 min/creator/month to manage), so the machine scales — the failure points are quality, not throughput.

TikTok Creator Marketplace waits for the owned-supply phase. Exceptions budget: 10–15% of activations need a human — the MDE ops seat, and the reason opex is $2K/client, not $1.5K.

05 THE ONLY BUILD

The Creative Loop

Win rate is defended, not assumed. The pre-mortem ("videos were bad") dies or lives here. Nothing on the market closes the loop from ad performance back to individual creator briefs — this is the one justified build, and it is assembly, not invention: the AdPros Creative Matrix and brief-builder already exist; Northbeam and the creative-analysis stack already cover the data side for tracked clients.

What a creator receives before recording: the brief (angle + hook bank + reference ads), the spec, and the product. What happens after Meta speaks: winners get scaled and their angle re-briefed across the roster; losers get one re-brief, then cut. At 16% win rate the same revenue needs 750 videos instead of 600 — a 25% supply burden the loop exists to prevent.
