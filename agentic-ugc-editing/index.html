    Agentic UGC Editing

    Tools, cost breakdown, build path and FAQ · Internal briefing

    The short version

    Editing is the bottleneck now, not footage

    AI generation made raw creator footage cheap. The edit didn't get cheaper. Every UGC ad still passes through a human timeline, and that is where our creative volume caps out. This page lays out what agentic editing is, who sells it, what it costs at our scale, and what we should do about it.

    DefinitionAgentic editing means an AI reads raw footage, decides what to keep, assembles a timeline, and hands back a rough cut. It is not text-to-video generation and it is not auto-captioning. The agent makes editorial decisions.
    01 Market

    The tools, and which ones actually fit us

    The category splits into five segments. Only two solve our problem; the rest are adjacent and easy to confuse with it.

    A

    Agentic workflow canvases — Mosaic

    Build a reusable edit graph once, run it on autopilot, A/B multiple variants from the same raw footage. REST API and webhooks. The only tool that natively does one shoot to many variants. YC W25, $3.8M raised. Entry plan reported around $50/mo with a 120-minute upload cap; confirm pricing directly, it is not publicly listed.

    B

    Pro rough-cut agents — Eddie AI

    Raw footage in, structured rough cut out, round-tripped to Premiere, Resolve, Final Cut and Avid. 50,000+ users. Now ships a headless SDK, a CLI and roughly 100 MCP tools callable from Claude. Pay-as-you-go credits that never expire: $10 per 1,000 up to $350 per 50,000. Import runs about 500 credits per hour of source. 1,000 free credits on signup.

    C

    Agent + NLE scripting — DaVinci Resolve 21.1

    Resolve now has native AI editing with Claude and ChatGPT plus a full Python API. Free, or $295 one-time for Studio. Footage stays on our machines. This is the build path described below.

    D

    Chat-directed editors — Valmera, Cardboard, Descript Underlord, Loopdesk

    Describe the edit in plain English on uploaded footage. Good UX, but one deliverable per request and no variant matrix. Subscriptions from $15 to $120/mo with weekly and monthly usage caps that agency volume will hit.

    E

    Not the same job — OpusClip, Reap, Klap, Creatify, Arcads

    Clipping tools pick moments out of finished video; they do not assemble from raw. Generation tools make synthetic footage; they do not edit ours. Both are useful, neither is agentic editing.

    MCP is the standard

    Eddie, Descript, Valmera and Reap all publish MCP access. Anything we build should assume MCP as the interface, which also makes switching vendors cheap.

    Metered beats capped

    Eddie and TwelveLabs meter usage. Mosaic, Cardboard and Valmera cap it. Our volume is lumpy; a shoot-heavy month blows through caps.

    Nobody scores against ROAS

    Every tool stops at producing the cut. Joining variants to Northbeam and Triple Whale data is unclaimed ground and the only part that compounds.

    03 Architecture

    The build path: agent + Resolve

    If we own the pipeline, this is the shape. The agent never clicks buttons; it calls Resolve's API with an edit decision list. Deterministic, inspectable, and the expensive GPU step runs once per shoot.

    1

    Footage bucket

    Creators upload once to an S3 or R2 bucket per client. Nothing ever moves through a chat window.

    2

    Understand

    Whisper transcribes, Gemini or TwelveLabs tags moments: hook, demo, objection, proof, CTA. Output is a timestamped table.

    3

    Decide

    Claude reads the brief and our creative rubric, picks clips, writes an EDL as JSON: source, in, out, track, order. This is where our IP lives.

    4

    Resolve on a GPU VM

    Resolve's Python API builds the timeline from the EDL, drops the client brand kit, renders a master cut. Spin the VM up per batch, kill it after. About $1/hr.

    5

    Verify

    The agent exports a frame strip, reviews it, and fixes obvious misses before a human sees anything.

    6

    Hand-off and fan-out

    Master cut and project file go to review (Frame.io, Drive or Slack). Approve sends it to Remotion for 10–30 variants on cheap CPU. Fix opens a 90%-done timeline for an editor, and the diff is logged as rubric training data.

    7

    Scored library

    Every clip and variant lands in Supabase with its performance joined from Northbeam and Triple Whale. The Decide step gets smarter every month. No vendor sells this.

    Why Resolve, not Premiere

    Adobe's per-named-user licensing restricts installs on shared or virtual servers without an enterprise agreement. Ten Premiere instances on rented VMs is a legal conversation. Resolve is free, Studio is $295 perpetual, and its Python API is more complete.

    Why not a computer-use agent

    Tools like Grok Bot run on a cloud microVM with no GPU and no local footage. Driving an NLE through screenshots is the slowest, most fragile interface to video. Every NLE we care about has an API; use it.

    04 Decision

    What we should do

    Now
    Run a two-week pilot

    Same creator shoot through Eddie and Mosaic. Score both on the eval checklist below. Under $200 all in. Log every edit our team makes after the agent's cut.

    Regardless
    Start the clip library

    Transcription plus tagging into Supabase is cheap, useful whichever tool wins, and the asset compounds from day one.

    After the pilot
    Decide build vs buy from data

    The edit log tells us which part of the edit agents actually fail at. Build the rubric there. Rent the editing until the gap is proven.

    Pilot eval checklist

    Score 1–5 or pass/fail per tool. Same footage, same brief, same week.

    • Hook selection: strongest opening 2 seconds?
    • Meaning preserved: no altered claims, no invented speech
    • Cut quality: clipped words, awkward gaps
    • Captions: names, numbers, timing on the rendered file
    • Framing: face and demo visible in 9:16
    • Revision control: change one thing, preserve the rest
    • Brand kit fidelity without manual reapplication
    • Variants: 10 from one source without 10 manual runs
    • Time to accepted final, and active review minutes
    • Cost per accepted ad, the only cost number that matters
    05 Questions

    FAQ

    Is this replacing our editors?

    No. It replaces starting from raw footage. Editors move to reviewing and adjusting a 90%-done timeline, which is where 1.5 hours per ad becomes 10–20 minutes. Their judgement becomes the rubric the agent learns from.

    Why not just use Creatify or Arcads and skip editing entirely?

    Those generate synthetic footage; they do not edit real creator footage. One practitioner test put AI UGC at 2.3–2.7% CTR versus 3.0–3.5% for human UGC. Synthetic wins on volume and loses on top-end performance. Different tool, different job.

    Why buy first if building is cheaper per ad?

    Because the gap is $2 per ad and we do not yet know where the agent breaks. Running 20 real jobs through a bought tool tells us which part is worth owning. You cannot spec a rubric for a failure mode you have not watched happen.

    What is an EDL and why does it matter?

    An edit decision list: a machine-readable record of which clip, which frames, which track, in which order. It is the contract between the agent's decision and the render. You can diff two EDLs; you cannot diff two click-streams.

    Could Grok Bot or another computer-use agent just drive Premiere?

    Technically yes, practically no. It runs in a cloud microVM with no GPU and none of our footage, and it can only run one computer-use task per screen. YouTube traffic on this topic is entirely agent-plus-MCP into Resolve or Premiere, not screenshot-driving; several videos on that passed 150k–300k views in the last two weeks.

    Where does client footage live and who can see it?

    In our own bucket, one per client. Eddie and Mosaic require upload to their cloud; the Resolve build path keeps footage on infrastructure we control. That is a factor for clients with strict usage terms on creator content.

    What is the single biggest risk?

    That we over-invest in the editing layer. Adobe, Blackmagic and Avid own the timeline and are adding agents; assume the base layer commoditises within 18–24 months. Build the scoring rubric and the performance-joined library. Rent the editing.

    Where is the detailed research?

    A workbook with a 13-vendor comparison, pricing detail, a live cost model and a blank eval scorecard sits with Nehal. Sources and confidence ratings are on its last tab. Ask before circulating any price externally.
