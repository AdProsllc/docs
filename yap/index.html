SOP — The STARR Method

Use this 5-beat structure for any solo, camera-direct Reel/TikTok/Short where a founder or creator is talking to camera. Best for 45–60s clips. Each letter = one specific job. Cut the video into the same 5 beats and you've got the structure.

S

Shocking Open with a claim that stops the scroll.

Lead with the punchline, not the setup. Frame as a result, a contradiction, or a "rumors are true" reveal. The first 1.5 seconds decides whether the rest of your video gets watched.

"The rumors are true — I've solved yapping."

T

Text Hook Persistent on-screen title that reinforces the promise visually.

Stays on screen the full duration. Black pill or yellow accent, top-center, max 5–7 words. A subhead in parentheses sharpens the angle. Carries the message for the 80%+ of viewers watching with sound off.

"I CRACKED YAP CONTENT (New Viral Yap Method)"

A

Achievement One sentence of credibility — why should they keep watching you.

Quantify effort or outcome (volume, reps, results). Don't list credentials, cite the work. Specific is sticky; vague is skippable.

"It took me 250 videos studying this."

R

Roadmap Tell them exactly what they're about to learn. Name the framework.

Use an acronym or numbered list — gives the viewer a finish line so they stay till the end. Reveal the structure visually (animated framework card) as you say it. The framework name is the retention tool.

"I'm going to show you the 5-letter STARR method."

R

Recipe Walk through each step concretely. The "how."

One letter at a time, fill in the framework graphic in sync with the voiceover. Each letter = one specific, copyable instruction. Close by pointing at the video itself as proof: "this video used all five — that's how you yap like a pro."

Reveal letter → define → cross off → reveal next. Self-referential close.

Failure Modes to Avoid

  • Slow-build hook. "Hey guys, today I'm going to talk about…" is a death sentence. Skip the warm-up.
  • Unnamed framework. No acronym = no roadmap = no retention. The structure must have a memorable label.
  • All letters at once. Dumping the full framework upfront kills curiosity. Reveal progressively.
  • Captions that disappear without a graphic substitute. Viewer needs something on screen at all times.
  • Generic credibility. "I'm an expert in X" is weaker than "I shot 250 videos to learn this."
  • No self-referential close. The strongest proof is the video itself. Always point back.

1. The hook is the entire game

Research from Meta's Reels for Business hub shows the strongest-performing reels land their hook inside the first 1.5 seconds. After three seconds, scroll velocity wipes out anything that hasn't earned attention. Hook rate (3-second views ÷ impressions) sits upstream of every downstream metric — CTR, conversion, ROAS. A 30%+ hook rate on Meta signals strong creative; below 15% means the first frame is losing the auction before the offer is even shown.

Six hook archetypes that work

01 Shocking claim

"I've solved yapping." · Lead with the punchline, fight the urge to set up.

02 Result-first

"$0 to $4.2M in 6 months." · Show the outcome, then reverse-engineer how.

03 Contrarian

"Hashtags don't actually help your reach." · Flips a popular belief on its head.

04 Curiosity gap

"No one's talking about this — but it changes everything." · Tease a hidden truth.

05 Listicle promise

"3 ways to make your next video go viral." · Numbered = finite = finishable.

06 Pattern interrupt

Snap transition, sudden close-up, unexpected camera angle. Visual surprise that resets attention.

The PVSS hook formula (educational content) Proof · why listen to you → Value · what they walk away with → Structure · how it's organized → Stakes · why it matters now. Maps cleanly onto STARR's Achievement + Roadmap beats.

2. Captions and on-screen text are non-negotiable

LinkedIn data: 80% of video views happen with sound off. Meta's own number is closer to 12% sound-off on Reels specifically, but in public/commute contexts it spikes to 69%. The asymmetry: a video that works without sound also works with sound. The reverse is not true.

Burned-in captions (open)

Always render. Full creative control over placement, font, color, animation. Used by every top-performing creator. Default for Ad Pros.

Auto-captions (closed/SRT)

Better for SEO (text indexable). Generic styling, viewer can toggle off. Use as backup layer behind burned-in for accessibility.

Caption mechanics

  • Safe zone. Keep text between 20% from top and 75% from top. Top 14% and bottom 20% get covered by platform UI (username, like button, caption field).
  • Line length. Under 32 characters per line. Two lines max on screen at once.
  • Display time. Minimum 1.2s per caption. For longer phrases, target 160–180 wpm reading speed (~2.5–3s for a 10-word line).
  • Contrast. White text + thin black stroke OR white pill background. Never yellow on white. Never light gray on light backgrounds.
  • Highlight emphasis. Color one or two key words per phrase (yellow on white, or pop-color on neutral) to anchor the eye.

3. Pacing and visual rhythm

Talking-head reels die when nothing changes for more than 3–4 seconds. The fix isn't faster talking — it's faster visual changes. Drop in a graphic, zoom, cutaway, or text pop-up every 10–15 seconds, especially in the first 20 seconds.

The 0–60s rhythm map

0–3sHook + text overlay appears. No talking-head-only frames.
3–10sEstablish credibility (Achievement). Visual change at 5s — graphic, zoom, or B-roll.
10–15sReveal framework name (Roadmap). Animate the framework card in.
15–50sWalk through each beat. New letter every 5–8s. Build the graphic progressively.
50–58sPull back to talking head. Self-referential close ("this video used all 5").
58–60sCTA + visual cue (wave, peace sign, fade). Loopable ending boosts replay rate.

4. Algorithm signals that matter in 2026

Watch time + completion rate

Heaviest weighted signal across TikTok, Reels, Shorts. A 30s reel watched fully beats a 60s reel watched halfway. Don't fill time to feel "complete."

3-second hold rate

Above 60% = exponential reach lift. Below 40% = creative dead on arrival. The single highest-leverage metric to optimize.

Loop rate

Reels that loop seamlessly get watched 2–3× per impression. End on the same frame/energy as the open. Don't fade to black.

Saves > likes

Saves and shares are weighted higher than likes in 2026. Educational/SOP content saves better than entertainment. STARR-style content is save-bait.

5. Posting cadence and platform fit

  • TikTok / Reels: 3–5×/week is the sustainable baseline. A steady 3×/week beats burst-then-silence patterns. Volume matters less than consistency.
  • YouTube Shorts: 2–3×/week. Algorithm rewards channels that post frequently — more uploads = more signals to learn from.
  • Mix: 70% evergreen (frameworks, SOPs, teaching), 30% trend-based (sounds, formats, current events). Evergreen compounds; trends decay.
  • Don't cross-post identical creative. Reframe the hook for each platform. TikTok rewards rawness; Reels reward polish; Shorts reward density.
