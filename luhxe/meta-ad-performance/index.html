Remaining caveats

Read before acting on any single cut.

Fulfillment cost missing: shipping, handling and fulfillment all read $0 in Triple Whale. Contribution margin computes to 77.3% of gross sales, putting breakeven NC ROAS at 1.29×. If fulfillment runs 8–12% of net sales — normal for apparel at a $204 AOV — true breakeven moves to 1.45–1.52×, and the 1.50× floor becomes a breakeven line rather than a success line.

Two spend sources, not fully reconciled: the tier tables use Triple Whale pixel-tracked spend (what a customer’s browser reported); the coverage tables above use Meta-reported spend via the warehouse. They differ by 3–15% per ad because pixel tracking misses some clicks. Directionally identical, not penny-identical.

The “– Copy” suffix

A small but important naming quirk covered on the Iteration tab.

Read: 57 names (4.1%) end in a bare "– Copy" or "– Copy N" suffix — Meta's own duplicate-ad naming, not part of the Luhxe schema. These are exact or near-exact re-launches of an existing concept into a new campaign, and because the base name is otherwise identical, they roll up together in every cut in this report. Where a "Copy" row and its original diverge in performance (see Iteration → Duplicated Names), it is worth checking whether the duplicate is running different targeting rather than assuming it is the same ad twice.

What this means for reading this report

Three practical takeaways.

Read: No tag is complete. Every tagged cut in this report (Angle, Format, Awareness, Sophistication, Dimension, Producer) covers a minority of ad names by count, though a majority of spend, because spend concentrates in the tagged, more mature creative. An untagged row in any table is not missing data — it is a real, usually older or simpler ad that predates or skipped the schema.

Spelling and casing drift are normalized, not original. "Problem-Aware" and "Problem Aware", "Identity" and "Identification", a bare "4" and "SL-4" — this report treats these as the same value. The underlying ad names in Meta still carry the original inconsistent spelling.

The schema evolved over the account's life. Older ads (P1, P2 concepts in the low hundreds) are simpler and less tagged; newer ads (P7, P8, the July-4th and Summer Flash Sale offer batches) follow the fuller convention more consistently. A cross-cohort comparison should account for this rather than assuming naming discipline was constant throughout.
