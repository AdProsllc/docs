    Half the account cannot be cut strategically.The full tagging schema resolves on roughly 40% of ads. The untagged legacy pool is $1.3–1.5M depending on the cut, and it consistently outperforms the tagged pool.     The API returned 100 of 2,895 ad names.Triple Whale truncates the ad-name breakdown at 100 rows sorted by spend, with no pagination parameter. Those rows cover $2,425,225 of $2,917,211 — 83.1% of Meta spend. The remaining $492K sits across 2,795 names, none spending more than $3,795. A $1,000 or $2,500 floor is therefore unreachable through this endpoint: both return the same 100 rows. Reaching the true tail needs a direct warehouse pull.
    Launch cohort is unusable in this window.81 of 100 ads report a first event date of Jul 26 — the first day of the reporting window. The field is censored at the window boundary, not a real launch date. A cohort cut needs a longer lookback, so it is omitted here rather than shown as a false signal.
    Fulfillment cost is missing from the P&L.Shipping, handling, and fulfillment all read $0 in Triple Whale. Contribution margin computes to 77.3% of gross sales, putting breakeven NC ROAS at 1.29×. If fulfillment runs 8–12% of net sales — normal for apparel at a $204 AOV — true breakeven moves to roughly 1.45–1.52×, and the 1.50× floor in this report becomes a breakeven line rather than a success line. This single input decides whether S1 means winning or barely covering costs.
    Attribution model matters.Everything here is Triple Attribution on a lifetime window. The same 30 days under Northbeam Clicks Only / 7-day put Meta at 1.09× first-time ROAS and $207 new CAC, against 1.95× and $117.78 here. Neither is wrong; they answer different questions. Never score an ad in this report against a target set in the other model.
