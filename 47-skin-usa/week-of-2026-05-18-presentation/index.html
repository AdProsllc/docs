What happened

●

Spend held flat at $40K (+1% WoW)

Budget was essentially unchanged, so the movement is efficiency, not scale.

●

NC ROAS fell to 0.55 (−24%)

New-customer orders dropped 438 → 322 and NC revenue fell $29K → $22K.

●

NC CPA rose to $125.91 (+37%)

We paid more per new customer for fewer of them, even though CTR ticked up to 4.8%.

02 Meta · diagnosis

Why it happened

●

Engagement held, conversion didn't

CTR rose to 4.8% while NC ROAS fell. The hook is landing the click; the drop is downstream at conversion.

●

Spend concentrated in mid-ROAS video

C018 absorbed ~$4.6K at 0.66 NC ROAS — volume leaned on proven video below target.

●

Cause under investigation

Tie the conversion drop to a specific campaign, audience, or LP change. Flagged, not assumed.

Next week

1

Audit the post-click path on top video

Pull LP and checkout conversion for C018 traffic to locate the drop.

2

Rebalance toward efficient angles

Shift budget out of sub-0.6 ROAS variants into angles above target.

3

Hold Meta spend until efficiency recovers

No scale until NC ROAS climbs back toward 0.7+.

What we learned

CTR is not the bottleneck

Creative is earning the click; focus optimization on post-click — offer, LP, audience.

Flat spend masked a volume drop

NC orders fell 26%. Watch order volume, not just spend, as the pacing signal.

03 Google · diagnosis

Why it happened

●

Prior week was a peak, not the baseline

2.48 NC ROAS was well above Google's ~1.5–1.8 norm. This week is closer to trend; the −43% is partly mean-reversion.

●

Branded vs non-branded mix

A shift toward non-branded prospecting would raise CPA and lower NC ROAS. Worth confirming in-account.

What we learned

Google is the efficiency anchor

Even off-peak, 1.42 NC ROAS beats Meta 3:1. There may be room to scale Google's share of budget.

Next week

1

Test a controlled budget increase

Trial a 15–20% lift on Google and watch NC CPA.

2

Segment branded vs non-branded

Report the two separately so efficiency swings are explainable.
