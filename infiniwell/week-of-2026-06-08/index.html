    What happened

    ●

    Week over week looks flat, but Meta stepped up at June 6

    The $492 to $521 move looks small because both weeks sit after the June 6 jump. Against the clean week before June 6, Meta nCAC is up about 40 percent (see the Northstar table).

    ●

    Clicks halved and cost per click doubled

    Link clicks went 31,924 to 14,202 and CPC went $0.97 to $2.15, on flat spend and flat impressions.

    ●

    New customers about even

    New customers were 63 the prior week and 58 last week, so volume held while cost per click rose.

    Why it happened

    ●

    The click drop is the planned Audience Network cut

    We pulled Audience Network off the quiz and PDP ad sets on June 8 to remove junk clicks. That placement posts inflated clicks, so removing it dropped reported CTR and raised CPC. It was running at 0.39 ROAS, so cutting it was the right call and did not change new customer volume much.

    ●

    The June 6 step-up cause is still being confirmed

    The nCAC step-up at June 6 lines up with the business-wide rise. The June 2 quiz URL change is one suspect, but the quiz split test is not yet routing traffic, so we will read it with a clean test before drawing a conclusion.

    Flexible ads with BPC and purchase exclusions, running from the Infiniwell page.

    BPC blocked on Google Shopping. With Shopping off, BPC PMax pushed budget into Discover, which spent far more for barely more sales. BPC carries Google, so clearing the block is the top priority.

    What happened

    ●

    BPC PMax nCAC nearly tripled

    BPC PMax went from $274 to $803 as the Shopping block forced budget into the Discover feed.

    ●

    The rest of Google held or improved

    Branded Shopping, NonBranded BPC and the Conquesting campaign all held or improved week over week.

    ●

    CEP launched on its own

    CEP now runs as its own campaign, separate from NMN, at an early nCAC of $182.

    Why it happened

    ●

    BPC is banned from Google Shopping, so PMax could no longer run Shopping ads

    PMax spreads one budget across Shopping, Search, Display, YouTube and the Discover feed. With Shopping blocked, that budget had to go somewhere, and the system pushed it into Discover.

    ●

    Budget shifted into the Discover feed, away from Search

    Discover went from a small slice of BPC PMax spend to more than half. At the same time, Search, our best converting placement, got cut almost in half.

    ●

    Same spend, about half the sales

    BPC PMax spent about the same per day across the two windows but brought in a little over half the new customers, so cost per sale climbed.
