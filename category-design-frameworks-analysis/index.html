April Dunford · Obviously Awesome (2019)

The five components, completed for Ad Pros.

Each component constrains the next. Read top to bottom.

1
Component 01 · Competitive Alternatives

What does an 8-figure operator do if Ad Pros doesn't exist?

Status quo (the real winner): they keep media in-house with a junior buyer or marketing manager, and accept the variance. This is the default Ad Pros has to beat first.

Generic performance agencies (Common Thread, Disruptive, Tier 11, dozens of mid-market shops): flat retainers $5K–$25K/mo, traditional team structure, indifferent to outcomes beyond the contract.

Freelance buyer + freelance creative roster: $3K–$8K/mo combined, no integration, all coordination falls on the operator.

Fractional CMO + outsourced execution: $10K–$15K/mo for strategy, separate vendors for buying and creative. Higher senior-eyes coverage but no production muscle.

Holding-company "growth" arms (WPP/Publicis/Stagwell digital units): expensive, slow, optimized for Fortune-500 not 8-figure DTC.

2
Component 02 · Differentiated Capabilities

What does Ad Pros have that none of the alternatives do?

CPA-linked variable pricing. $5K base + tiered kicker (4% / 3% / 2% / 1.5%) means Ad Pros gets paid more when the operator wins, less when they don't. No major alternative offers this — they bill flat.

Creator Performance Network with 7/3 split. Creators participate in performance economics. Brands buy a creator program where the agency, brand, and creators share upside. No major alternative offers performance-aligned creator economics.

The Intelligence Stack (AI Ads Library + Intel + Agentic Workflows / Noor): proprietary AI infrastructure processing 5K+ Meta ads/day, BigQuery + Supabase + Claude API stack. Most agencies have no in-house AI infrastructure — they use third-party SaaS.

9 Dimensions Creative Strategy + Creative Matrix + Breakthrough classification: a named creative scoring system with a breakthrough definition (5–10% of account spend, ROAS target, <90 days). Most agencies score creative ad-hoc.

11 channels managed by one team: Meta, Google, YouTube, AppLovin, TikTok, Native, Snap, Pinterest, CTV, Bing, LinkedIn, Reddit. No fee-per-platform. Most alternatives charge separately per channel or only cover 2–3.

Operator-grade case studies at scale: $4M/mo Kachava, $4M+/mo SupportPets revenue, $2M/mo AudienHearing, AG1 / Canada Goose / Infiniwell. Few alternatives have $1M+/mo managed-spend case studies in DTC.

3
Component 03 · Differentiated Value

What value do those capabilities deliver to an 8-figure operator?

Aligned incentives → lower variance. With CPA-linked pricing the agency earns more when the operator wins. Operators stop fighting the agency to produce more, test faster, scale harder — incentives are already pointing the same direction.

Performance creator economics → cheaper, better creator volume. CPN's 7/3 split lets creators earn upside instead of flat fees. This attracts a higher tier of creator at lower cost-per-asset, which compounds creative testing velocity.

AI infrastructure → faster breakthrough discovery. The Intelligence Stack means new winners are identified and scaled in days not weeks, and competitive intel is processed continuously not quarterly.

Named creative scoring → predictable breakthrough yield. Operators get one breakthrough ad per quarter at a minimum (Foundation tier) up to one per month (Breakthrough tier). Predictable creative ROI, not "we'll try."

Multi-channel under one team → no agency tax across channels. 11 channels, one team, one fee. Operators stop paying 3 agencies for 3 channels. Coordination overhead drops to zero.

4
Component 04 · Best-Fit Customer

Who cares enough about that value to pay partnership economics?

The Dream Client filter from the deck, sharpened for self-selection:

  • $3M+/mo revenue ($250K minimum monthly floor)
  • $1M+/mo paid spend ($100K minimum monthly floor)
  • AOV $50–$150 (mass-market DTC, not luxury, not subscription-only)
  • 30%+ revenue from repeat customers (LTV economics that justify premium fees)
  • Founder-led OR a CEO who personally reviews ad performance weekly
  • Has tried 2+ agencies before and fired them (knows the pain of vendor relationships)
  • Treats ad spend as a P&L line, not a marketing expense

Verticals that fit cleanly: health/supplements (Kachava, AG1, Infiniwell), pet (SupportPets), wellness tech (AudienHearing), apparel/lifestyle with strong repeat (Canada Goose).

Verticals that don't fit: B2B SaaS, single-purchase products with no repeat (mattresses, furniture), low-AOV ecommerce ($<$50), local services, anything below $500K/mo total spend.

5
Component 05 · Market Category

What category context makes the value obvious?

Frame Ad Pros inside the existing "performance marketing agency" category — buyers already understand the category and what they expect from it (media buying, creative, reporting). Don't try to invent a new category; the cost of educating the market is too high and the position is sharp enough without it.

Claim a sub-position inside that category: "performance partner" — the agency model where fees scale with operator outcomes. This is positioned against "performance vendor" (the default), which most agencies are even when they call themselves partners.

The category line: "Performance partner for 8-figure operators." Six words. Audience-first. Self-selecting. Reads like a job description that exists in the market, not a tagline an agency invented.

The recursion check Each component reinforces the next. Best-fit customer (8-figure operator with 30%+ repeat revenue) cares about lower variance and faster breakthrough discovery. That value comes from differentiated capabilities (CPA pricing, CPN, Intelligence Stack). Those capabilities only differentiate against the alternatives (status quo, generic agencies, freelancers). All of it lives inside the performance partner sub-category. The framework holds.
April Dunford · Sales Pitch (Sales Pitch, 2023)

The Ad Pros sales pitch, structured.

Dunford's pitch sequence builds on the 5 components. It's the order to walk a prospect through on a discovery call.

Step 1 · Insight

"Most 8-figure DTC brands have one thing in common: they've fired at least two agencies. The pattern isn't bad agencies — it's misaligned incentives. When the agency gets paid the same whether you scale or stall, the relationship rots."

Step 2 · Alternatives + Trade-offs

"The four ways operators handle this today: keep it in-house and accept the variance, hire a flat-fee agency, stitch together freelancers, or pay a fractional CMO who outsources execution. Each one trades off something — usually senior eyes for cost, or coordination for control."

Step 3 · The Different Approach

"There's a fifth option, and it's the one we built. Performance partner economics: low floor, kicker on spend, equity-like exposure for creators. We get paid more when you scale and less when you don't. Same direction, same incentives, no rotting."

Step 4 · The Capabilities (proof of different)

"Three things make this work. The Breakthrough System: a named creative scoring methodology that produces a quantified breakthrough ad every 30–90 days at 5–10% of account spend. The Creator Performance Network: 7% to us, 3% to creators, performance-aligned. And the Intelligence Stack: proprietary AI infrastructure processing 5,000+ ads a day so we find winners faster than anyone scoring creative manually."

Step 5 · The Value (what the operator gets)

"Net-net: lower variance, predictable breakthrough yield, faster scale, and no coordination overhead because all 11 channels run under one team. Operators we work with — Kachava at $4M/mo, SupportPets 10x'd to $4M+/mo, AudienHearing at $2M/mo on AppLovin and TikTok Shop — those numbers come from this structure, not despite it."

Step 6 · The Best-Fit Filter

"This isn't for everyone. We're built for operators doing $3M+/mo revenue with $1M+/mo in spend, AOV $50–$150, 30%+ repeat revenue, founder-led, and treating spend as a P&L line. If that's you, the math works. If you're earlier than that, we're not the right fit yet."

Step 7 · The Ask

"Want to look at the math on your account? We can walk through what partnership economics would have looked like on your last 90 days of spend, and you can decide if the structure makes sense."

The pitch order matters Most agencies pitch capabilities first ("we do creative + media + production"). Dunford's sequence pitches insight first, alternatives second, the different approach third, capabilities fourth. By the time capabilities arrive, the prospect already wants the different approach to be possible. Capabilities become proof, not features.
Play Bigger · Point of View

The Ad Pros POV, written out.

Four-part argument: (1) name the unnamed problem, (2) cost of not solving it, (3) the future where it's solved, (4) the inevitable solution.

Part 1 · The Problem

The agency model is structurally misaligned with operator economics — and operators know it but tolerate it because the alternatives are worse.

For every dollar an 8-figure DTC operator spends on a flat-retainer agency, the agency captures the same revenue whether spend scales 10x or sits flat. The agency makes more money by holding more accounts, not by making any single account win. This is the hidden tax inside every flat-retainer engagement: the people executing your media don't financially benefit when you grow, and don't financially suffer when you don't.

Operators have responded by churning agencies — the average 8-figure DTC brand fires 2–3 agencies before settling. They blame execution. The real issue is structure.

Part 2 · The Cost of Not Solving It

Misaligned incentives compound into variance, churn, and a permanent ceiling on what paid media can do for a brand.

When the agency optimizes for retention over results, three things happen: (1) testing slows because losing tests look bad and winning tests don't pay extra; (2) breakthrough creative becomes rare because breakthroughs require risk and the agency has nothing to gain from risk; (3) the senior team rotates off accounts as soon as they're stable, leaving juniors to manage spend that's now generating the agency's revenue floor.

The cost shows up as variance — months where ROAS swings unpredictably — and as a ceiling. Most 8-figure DTC brands plateau at $3–5M/mo spend not because the channels are saturated, but because the agency model can't push past it.

Part 3 · The Future

Performance partnership replaces vendor relationships across every layer of the agency model — media, creative, creators.

In 5 years, the operators winning at scale won't be paying agencies flat retainers. They'll be paying performance partners — agencies whose fee structure scales with outcomes, whose creators participate in upside instead of flat per-asset fees, and whose internal infrastructure is built on operator stack (BigQuery, AI agents, real-time attribution) instead of agency stack (Asana boards and slide decks).

The boundary between "operator" and "agency" blurs. The agency runs on the same infrastructure the operator runs on, gets paid on the same terms the operator gets paid on, and stays for years instead of quarters because the math compounds for everyone in the room.

Part 4 · The Inevitable Solution

Ad Pros is the performance partner for 8-figure operators — and the only agency that has restructured all three layers (media, creative, creators) on aligned economics.

Media: $5K base + variable kicker scaling 4% to 1.5% across spend tiers. The variable dominates at the spend levels we serve.

Creators: the Creator Performance Network — 7% kept, 3% passed to creators. Creators earn upside; brands buy performance-aligned creator programs.

Infrastructure: the Intelligence Stack — AI Ads Library, Intel, and Agentic Workflows running on BigQuery + Supabase + Claude API. Operator infrastructure, not agency infrastructure.

This is the only structure where the agency, the brand, and the creators all win when spend scales — and all share exposure when it doesn't. Performance partnership isn't a tagline. It's a fee structure, an org structure, and an infrastructure stack. We built all three.

Category Pirates · Magic Triangle

Position–Product–Company fit, audited.

Three sides, equal weight. If any one drifts, the position weakens. Adapted from Pirates' Magic Triangle — Position substituted for Category since Ad Pros is repositioning, not creating a category.

Side 1 · Position

Performance partner for 8-figure operators

What the position promises: aligned economics. Floor + kicker. Equity-like creator exposure. Operator-grade infrastructure.

Where it lives: website hero, deck cover, sales script, IG bios, LinkedIn taglines.

Position is sharp ✓
Side 2 · Product

Services + pricing structure

What's right: the kicker structure exists (4% / 3% / 2% / 1.5%). CPN exists (7/3 split). Intelligence Stack exists internally. 9 Dimensions exists.

What's misaligned: in the sample proposal, variable is only 37% of total fees — the kicker is the garnish, not the entrée. Production is sold standalone (commodity-priced). CPN is "coming soon" instead of lead. Intelligence Stack is buried.

Product partially fits — pricing weight is the gap
Side 3 · Company

Team + infrastructure + culture

What's right: 26 team members across 7 countries. AI infrastructure (BigQuery, Supabase, Claude API, Noor). Senior operators (Katherine, Pedro, Chino, Dani named in past work).

What needs reinforcement: the team needs to see itself as "operators not account managers." Internal language, performance reviews, hiring criteria all need to reinforce the partner identity. Currently strong on infrastructure, less codified on culture-as-position-evidence.

Company largely fits — codify the culture layer

The single point of failure

Pirates' rule: of the three, the weakest side determines the strength of the whole. Right now the weakest side is Product — specifically the pricing structure. The position promises performance partnership; the product (proposal) bills 63% fixed. Until variable dominates, the triangle has a structural gap that any sophisticated operator will catch within five minutes of reading the proposal.

Action: close the Product side Restructure the standard partnership proposal so variable is 65–80% of total fees. Reserve current structure as the explicit "vendor tier" for non-partnership engagements. The triangle holds when the product matches the rhetoric — not before.

Category Pirates · The 22 Laws of Category Design

The 5 laws that matter, applied to Ad Pros.

Read the rest. Internalize these five. Each one has a concrete Ad Pros action.

Law 1 · The Different Law

Don't compete on better. Win on different.

Ad Pros action: stop saying "we're the best performance agency for DTC." Start saying "we're the only agency where the fee structure scales with operator outcomes." First is a benchmark claim (every agency makes it). Second is a structural claim (only Ad Pros can prove it).

Law 2 · The Niche Down Law

Become known for one thing before you expand.

Ad Pros action: the one thing is "performance partnership." Not "performance creative" or "AI media buying" or "creator economics" alone — the integrated economics across all three layers. Resist the temptation to also be known for AI infrastructure or creator networks separately. Those are proof points, not the niche.

Law 3 · The Languaging Law

The words you choose shape the category.

Ad Pros action: three names installed everywhere — "performance partner," "8-figure operator," "Creator Performance Network." Audit every public artifact within one week. Saturate, don't drip.

Law 4 · The Damming Demand Law

Dam existing demand and redirect it to your category.

Ad Pros action: existing demand is "I need an agency." Dam it: convert that demand into "I need a performance partner." Every piece of outbound, every podcast, every IG post should reframe the buyer's question — not "which agency?" but "vendor or partner?" Once that frame catches on, Ad Pros is the only credible answer.

Law 5 · The 6-10 Law (with caveat)

It takes 6–10 years to design and dominate a category.

Ad Pros caveat: the full law applies to category creation. Ad Pros isn't creating a category — it's claiming a sub-position inside performance agencies. Realistic horizon: 18–24 months to be the recognized partnership-pricing leader, 36 months to be the default answer when an 8-figure operator says "I need a real partner." Don't let the 6–10 law license slow execution; do let it license multi-year patience for compounding effects.

Play Bigger · Define-Develop-Dominate

Where Ad Pros sits in the lifecycle.

Three phases. Knowing the phase dictates strategy.

1
Phase 1 · DEFINE

Where the category is right now

"Performance partnership agency" as a defined sub-category does not yet exist. Several agencies use partnership language loosely (Common Thread, Pilothouse, a few smaller shops have variable kicker components). None has claimed the category as its identity. The market currently sees them all as "performance agencies with optional kickers."

Implication: the Define phase is where Ad Pros sits today. The opportunity is to anchor the problem ("flat-retainer agencies are misaligned") and the language ("performance partner") in the market's mind before any competitor does.

2
Phase 2 · DEVELOP

Where the category goes next (12–24 months)

If Ad Pros executes the Lightning Strike and the post-strike steady drip, the Develop phase will feature 2–3 agencies competing for the partnership-positioning lane: Ad Pros plus likely Pilothouse and one other emerging shop. This is where weaker entrants drop out — agencies that try to copy partnership pricing without restructuring their economics will fail to deliver on the promise and lose the operators who signed up.

Implication: the goal for the next 18 months is to establish Ad Pros as the unambiguous Develop-phase leader. That's measured by inbound: when an 8-figure operator types "performance partnership agency" into a search bar or asks a peer for a referral, Ad Pros is the first name spoken.

3
Phase 3 · DOMINATE

The 36-month destination

In a fully formed sub-category, the leader takes 60–80% of category economics — meaning 60–80% of the operators specifically seeking a performance partner choose Ad Pros. Other agencies in the lane survive but never lead. Total addressable market in the 8-figure DTC partnership lane is plausibly 200–500 brands globally. Capturing 20–30% of that as managed accounts at $1M+/mo spend would be a $200M+ ARR business with industry-leading margins.

Implication: Dominate phase requires defending the position once held. The defense moves: deeper integration with operator stack (BigQuery, attribution, finance), exclusive Intelligence Stack capabilities, public manifesto-grade content cadence, and a CPN with creator network effects competitors can't replicate.

The strategic call Ad Pros is in Define. Most agencies miss this — they think they're in Develop or Dominate when they're actually still in Define. Recognizing the phase is what justifies the Lightning Strike and the manifesto: in Define, anchoring the problem and the language is more valuable than any individual feature or case study. Anchor first. Develop second. Dominate later.
