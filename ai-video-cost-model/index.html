02 How to read it

Four things the model shows

1

Gens per scene is the biggest lever

Moving from ten takes per scene to four roughly halves total cost and can drop the GPU requirement to a single card. This is a creative-quality question, not a hardware one — measure the real usable-take rate before assuming you need ten.

2

The cheapest GPU per hour is rarely the cheapest per video

What matters is dollars per hour divided by throughput. Slow cards win on paper and lose in practice, because hitting volume means running six pods instead of one. Always read the Cards column next to the cost.

3

Editing cost does not move when generation cost falls

A reviewer takes the same time on a hundred videos whether each cost four dollars or forty cents to produce. As generation is optimised down, the human share climbs on its own. Watch the two percentages flip.

4

Throughput is the one assumption worth measuring

The GPU seconds per output second slider drives the entire generation line. The default of seventeen comes from a single published benchmark, not our hardware. One timed generation on the pod settles it, and every number downstream shifts proportionally.

02 Trade-offs

What the cheaper rate costs you

1

No uptime guarantee

Marketplace and spot instances carry no service level agreement and can be reclaimed with fifteen seconds notice. Jobs must checkpoint. Segment-based video generation does this naturally, so an interrupted run loses one segment rather than a batch.

2

Host location is not reliably compliant

Machines are listed by individual hosts and the physical location varies. There is no data processing agreement available. Anything involving client brand assets, unreleased products or personal data should stay on an SLA-backed provider with a known jurisdiction.

3

Setup is repeated per instance

Without a persistent network volume shared across pods, each new instance re-downloads model weights. At sixty to eighty gigabytes per environment this is a real time cost, and it argues for baking models into a container image before running at volume.

4

Prices float with supply and demand

Marketplace rates are set by hosts and move continuously. The figures modelled here were accurate on 17 August 2026 and should be re-checked before any sustained commitment. Reserved tiers on fixed-price providers trade a small premium for rate certainty.

Where the compute should actually run

Correction to an earlier version of this model RunPod Community Cloud was modelled at $0.50/hr. The verified rate is $0.69/hr, so earlier Community-tier figures were understated by roughly 38 per cent. The Secure Cloud rate of $0.99/hr is confirmed correct.

How RunPod prices

Two clouds, one platform

Community Cloud runs on third-party hardware with no uptime guarantee and pods that can be preempted. Secure Cloud is RunPod’s own infrastructure with a 99 per cent SLA. The gap between them is roughly thirty per cent.

What stacks on top

Network volume storage runs five to seven cents per gigabyte per month, container disk ten cents per gigabyte while the pod is live. Billing is per second on pods. Serverless bills per second of execution with no idle charge.

Recommendation

1

Build on RunPod, batch on the marketplace

Keep RunPod for development and for any workload touching client data — it has EU datacenters and will sign a data processing agreement, which marketplace providers cannot reliably offer. Move bulk overnight generation to Vast.ai or a spot tier, where the per-video cost falls by roughly two thirds.

2

Preemption is not a real risk for this workload

Generation is queued and segment-based, so an interrupted job re-queues the unfinished segment rather than losing the batch. Spot and marketplace tiers carry no uptime guarantee and can reclaim an instance on fifteen seconds notice, which matters for training runs and not for batch inference that checkpoints naturally.

3

Check the consumer licence before standardising on a 5090

The RTX 5090 is a consumer GeForce card with no error-correcting memory and is subject to Nvidia’s consumer licence, which may restrict datacenter use. The L40S and RTX PRO 6000 are the datacenter-licensed alternatives and are worth confirming with counsel before a commercial pipeline depends on the cheaper card.

4

Provider choice is a second-order decision

Every option in the table beats the hosted-API baseline by an order of magnitude. Moving from the most expensive tier to the cheapest saves around eight hundred dollars a month; moving off hosted APIs saves several thousand. Make the platform decision on data residency and operational fit, then optimise the rate.
