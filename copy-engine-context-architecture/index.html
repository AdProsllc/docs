Copy Engine

Context is the product

The ultimate copy engine for ecommerce brands is not a better prompt — it is a better context architecture. Eight context layers feed a single joined context pack, the pack powers generation, and every ad that ships is tagged with the context it was built from so results flow back into the corpus. The system learns; competitors’ tools just write.

The join is the product

Everyone scrapes. Almost nobody joins voice-of-customer language to spend-validated ad structures to the brand’s claimable mechanism. That three-way join — the context pack — is what makes generated copy convert instead of merely exist.

The moat is the right column

The left column is scrapeable by any competitor with the same tools. The right column — our ad performance data, sales conversations, tested failures, and synthesized avatars — comes from running real spend. It compounds and cannot be copied.

The loop makes it learn

Every ad ships tagged with its source skeleton, VOC phrase, and claim. Results flow back into the corpus. Without the loop this is a research tool; with it, the engine gets measurably better with every dollar of client spend.

Bottom line Phase 1 requires almost no new build — it re-points infrastructure Ad Pros already runs (Media Intelligence Pipeline, AdScan, Scrape Creators, Pulse agents, BigQuery) into one joined context pack per brand. The differentiator is architecture and discipline, not new tooling spend.
Layer Functions

What each layer does

Each layer answers one question a world-class copywriter would ask before writing a line. Together they replace weeks of manual research with a continuously refreshed brand brain.

Scraped external

1. Brand context

Question it answers
Who are we and what can we truthfully claim?
Inputs
Top 50–100 site pages, PDPs, advertorials, email/SMS flows, on-site reviews, offer history, catalog and margins.
Function
Produces the claims library, tone-of-voice fingerprint, and the unique mechanism definition — the single most transplantable asset the brand owns.
Stack
Site crawl, Klaviyo, Shopify, BigQuery.
Scraped external

2. Competitor context

Question it answers
What is already working around us, and where is the whitespace?
Inputs
Competitor ad libraries weighted by longevity, their landing pages, 1–2 star reviews, pricing and promo cadence, email flows.
Function
Separates spend-validated winners from noise; maps angles, avatars, and mechanisms no competitor is claiming.
Stack
AdScan / Ad Manage, Scrape Creators ad library endpoints, Pulse Offer and Messaging agents.
Scraped external

3. Organic content context

Question it answers
What earns attention in this niche without paid spend?
Inputs
Outlier videos (views vs. channel median) across IG, TikTok, YouTube; transcripts; comment sections; adjacent-niche winners tagged by persuasion skeleton.
Function
Supplies proven hook shapes and story arcs for cross-niche mechanism transplants before they ever hit paid.
Stack
Scrape Creators profile/video/transcript endpoints, Supabase outlier scoring.
Scraped external

4. Market and news context

Question it answers
What is the customer conversation right now, in their own words?
Inputs
Category-wide Amazon reviews, Reddit and forum threads, rising search queries, category studies and news moments, seasonality.
Function
The canonical voice-of-customer dataset: desired outcomes, failed solutions, trigger events, objections — ranked by frequency.
Stack
Scrape Creators Reddit endpoints, web research, Google Trends.
Proprietary moat

5. Ad performance context

Question it answers
What has spend already validated — and what has it already killed?
Inputs
Every ad plus transcript and creative attributes joined to ROAS, CPA, hook rate, hold rate; fatigue curves; the negative corpus of tested losers; cross-client patterns.
Function
Grounds generation in results, not vibes. The negative corpus prevents re-testing known losers — survivorship-bias insurance no competitor can scrape.
Stack
Media Intelligence Pipeline, BigQuery, Northbeam, Triple Whale, 9-Dimensions framework.
Proprietary moat

6. Audience context

Question it answers
Who exactly are we persuading, and how aware are they?
Inputs
Avatars synthesized from review mining, comments, surveys, and zero-party quiz data — never invented from thin air.
Function
Maps awareness stage and objection hierarchy per avatar, plus category sophistication drift — which determines which hooks are even eligible.
Stack
Pulse Avatar agent, layers 3–4 as raw material.
Proprietary moat

7. Conversation context

Question it answers
Where does the promise break after the click?
Inputs
Sales call transcripts, support tickets, live chat, cancellation reasons, mid-funnel ad comments on our own running ads.
Function
Captures objections and desire language at the moment of persuasion and the moment of churn — the most under-mined copy source in DTC.
Stack
Zoom, Pylon, Scrape Creators comment endpoints.
Constraints

8. Compliance context

Question it answers
What are we not allowed to say?
Inputs
Platform policy per vertical, FTC/FDA claim boundaries, brand guardrails, the historical ad-rejection log.
Function
Filters generation before launch so approved volume stays high and accounts stay safe — especially for supplement and health clients.
Stack
Policy rules encoded per client, rejection log in ClickUp/Supabase.

The three system components

A

Context pack

The structured join per brand: claims table, VOC phrase bank, skeleton library, avatar map, constraint rules. Stored as queryable schema — not raw dumps — so Pulse agents pull exactly what a brief needs. Each source carries a freshness TTL: ads weekly, VOC quarterly, site on-change.

B

Copy engine

Generation grounded in the pack: hooks, scripts, advertorials, and cross-niche mechanism transplants. Enforces funnel congruence — hook, landing page, offer, and email must message-match — and checks angle saturation before recommending a skeleton the market is about to fatigue on.

C

Feedback loop

Every generated ad ships tagged with its source context — skeleton, VOC phrase, claim. Performance joins back to the tags, winners strengthen their patterns, losers enter the negative corpus. This is what turns a research tool into a learning system that compounds across the whole client book.

Rollout Plan

Lowest lift, highest impact first

Sequenced by return on effort. Phase 1 mostly re-points infrastructure we already run. Nothing in Phase 3 blocks Phase 1 — the engine produces usable copy from week one and gets smarter every phase after.

Phase 1 — Re-point what we already have (weeks 1–2)

1

Own ad performance corpus Low lift Highest impact

The Media Intelligence Pipeline and creative-analysis workflows already exist. Work is structuring output into the context pack schema per brand — not building ingestion. This is the single biggest unfair advantage and it is nearly free to activate.

2

Competitor longevity-weighted ads Low lift High impact

AdScan and Scrape Creators already pull competitor libraries. Add the 90-day longevity filter so the corpus is spend-validated winners, not everything that exists. Feeds the transplant SOP directly.

3

Ad comment mining Low lift High impact

Scrape Creators comment endpoints on our own and competitors’ running ads. Mid-funnel objections and desire language captured at the moment of persuasion — essentially free, and almost nobody in the industry mines it systematically.

4

Negative corpus Low lift Compounding

Start logging every killed ad and failed test with hypothesis and result from day one. Cheap to begin, impossible to backfill later, and unscrapable by anyone else. The earlier it starts, the bigger the moat.

Phase 2 — Build the pack and close the loop (weeks 3–6)

5

Site crawl + claims library Medium lift High impact

Top 50–100 pages per brand parsed into a claims table tagged by type and compliance risk, plus the unique mechanism definition. One-time crawl per brand, refreshed on-change.

6

VOC mining + avatar synthesis Medium lift High impact

Category-wide Amazon and Reddit mining ranked by frequency, synthesized into data-grounded avatars with awareness stages and objection hierarchies via the Pulse Avatar agent. Quarterly refresh — VOC is stable.

7

Feedback-loop tagging Medium lift Compounding

Naming convention plus schema so every launched ad carries its source skeleton, VOC phrase, and claim — joined to performance in BigQuery. The highest-leverage item in the entire plan; everything after it compounds.

8

Conversation context Medium lift Medium impact

Pipe Zoom sales calls, Pylon tickets, and cancellation reasons into the phrase bank. Depends on per-client data access, so runs client-by-client as access lands.

Phase 3 — Expand and defend (quarter two and beyond)

9

Organic outlier engine Higher lift Medium impact

The Sandcastles-style rebuild: niche watchlists, outlier scoring (views vs. channel median) in Supabase, transcript teardown feeding the skeleton library. Real build work — worth it once Phases 1–2 are producing.

10

Saturation index Higher lift Medium impact

Track how many advertisers are running each skeleton right now, so the engine recommends angles about to work — not angles about to fatigue. The difference between copying winners and copying corpses.

11

Compliance layer + market monitoring Low lift Protective

Encode per-vertical policy rules and the rejection log as a pre-launch filter; add news and trend monitoring for timely angles. Protective rather than generative — keeps volume high and accounts safe as output scales.

Sequencing logic Items 1–4 ship in two weeks because ingestion already exists — the work is joining, not building. Item 7 (feedback tagging) is deliberately mid-sequence: it needs the pack schema from items 5–6 to tag against, but everything launched after it makes the system permanently smarter. Phase 3 is expansion, not foundation — skip or defer any of it without breaking the engine.
