Meta Just Put A Price Tag On The Reach You Used To Get Free

Meta One is live: 50+ features across eight plans, from $2.99 to $499 a month. Here's what's actually in it, what it costs, and why only one tier changes how your organic performs.

NK
Nehal Kazim
Founder, Ad Pros

Meta has launched Meta One, a subscription that spans Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp and Meta AI. It is not one product — it is eight plans, and the gap between them is the whole story. The cheap ones sell expression: fonts, themes, reactions. The expensive ones sell things advertisers already fight over — feed placement, clickable links, follower growth, exportable data and a human being to call. Below is every plan, every price, and an honest read on which of it matters for your ad account.

Meta says the core experience across its apps and Meta AI stays free. It also says there are already 15 million subscriptions and trials, which tells you this is not a test balloon.

Read the pricing carefully

Every business tier is priced "starting at." Meta states that plans, benefits, pricing and availability vary by region, by app and by account — and the business tiers are priced per platform, per month. You will not know a client's real number until you open the subscription flow on their own account. Budget from the floor, quote from the flow.

§ Section 2

Enhanced Visibility

A dedicated spot in the feed — and in search.

EssentialAdvanced +ExpertMax

Subscribers get featured placement in a dedicated carousel inside people's feeds, shown to users Meta judges likely to be interested, plus optimized search placement — ranking higher in on-platform search on both Instagram and Facebook. Two surfaces that used to depend entirely on organic engagement or ad spend to earn.

This is the only new category in the whole product. Everything else in Meta One is either Meta Verified repackaged or a volume ladder. This is Meta selling distribution directly, and it unlocks at Advanced — $49.99/mo, not the $14.99 entry price.

Driving Traffic

Posts and Reels can carry a clickable link inside the content itself, removing the bio-link and swipe-up detour that has cost advertisers conversions for years. Essential adds an enhanced link sheet with images on profile and a custom searchable web URL for WhatsApp Business.

The catch: links are not unlimited, they are credits on a ladder that climbs with the tier.

⚠ The credit trap

Link credits cannot be applied retroactively to a post that's already published, and they are not recoverable if a post underperforms and gets archived. Spend a credit on a dud and it's gone. That turns link placement into a planning decision your content team has to make before publishing, not a lever you pull when something starts working.

§ Section 4

Audience Growth

Meta actively pushes people to follow you.

EssentialAdvanced +ExpertMax

A bold follow button on Reels, plus an automatic follow invitation sent to anyone who engages with a subscriber's content — turning passive engagement into audience growth without extra spend. Essential also brings the verified badge across Instagram, Facebook and WhatsApp (subject to verification) and proactive impersonation monitoring, with verified WhatsApp channels laddering 1 → 3 → 5 → 10 across the tiers.

Read this as a paid follower campaign with no CPM attached. Whether it beats a paid follower campaign on cost depends entirely on your existing engagement volume — the invite only fires at people who already interacted.

§ Section 5

Data & Reporting

The quiet one that actually changes your workflow.

EssentialAdvanced +ExpertMax
  • Exportable analytics — the difference between insight you can only read inside the app and data you can pull into a reporting stack. If you report to clients or a board, this one line is worth more than the visibility features.
  • Competitive insights (Instagram) — benchmark your profile against similar public accounts.
  • Custom audience insights (Facebook) — surfaces your most active followers and your newest fans.
  • Optimized scheduling (Facebook) and scheduling Stories up to 30 days out — auto-posting timed to peak audience activity.
  • Original content credit requests — ladder of 2 → 4 → 6 per month from Advanced up.

Worth noting

Some of this — search placement, a working support line — feels like it should already be standard. It isn't, and Meta isn't the first: X Premium and LinkedIn Premium ran the same playbook. Pay to remove friction the platform itself created.

The sharper tell is what Meta leaves out of its own announcement. The featured-feed carousel, search ranking, competitive insights, support case counts and seat counts appear nowhere in the launch post. Meta leads on expression and AI. The distribution-and-data features — the ones that actually move a client's numbers — you only find inside the product. That asymmetry is itself the story.

The inflection point

Six of the eight levers unlock at the same place: Advanced. Below it you are buying a badge and a link sheet. Above it you are buying more of the same — higher ceilings, not new capability. If Meta One is worth anything to your brand, it is worth exactly $49.99 per platform per month, and the Expert and Max decisions are volume questions, not strategy questions.

§ The shift

Why this matters more than a normal update

If your brand depends on Meta for customer acquisition, treat this like any other paid-channel decision. At $49.99 per platform per month, Advanced across Instagram and Facebook runs roughly $100/mo — about $1,200 a year. That is a rounding error next to a serious media budget and a real line item next to a small one.

So the question is not whether $100 is affordable. It is whether a feed carousel slot, a higher search rank, four link credits and one support case a month move your numbers more than $100 of additional ad spend would. For most accounts spending under $5k/mo, the honest answer is that the support case and the exportable analytics justify it and the visibility features are a bonus. Above $50k/mo, the visibility levers are the only part that scales.

Paying for advantages that used to be free isn't new — it's the same playbook platforms have run for years. The only question that matters is whether the specific levers on offer are worth it for your account.

§ The limits

What it doesn't fix

  • A subscription doesn't fix a weak offer or poor product-market fit — more visibility just shows more people something that isn't converting.
  • It doesn't replace paid media strategy. Feed and search placement complement your funnel; they don't run it.
  • The support line resolves account issues — it isn't a creative or media-buying resource, and at Advanced it's one case a month.
  • Link credits are a planning constraint, not a growth lever. Four Instagram links a month means four deliberate decisions, not four experiments.
  • Pricing is per platform. A brand running Instagram, Facebook and WhatsApp is making three subscription decisions, not one.
§ What's coming

What Meta says is next

  • Expansion beyond the four apps — Meta One extending to Edits and AI glasses over time.
  • Edits Plus — cloud storage for syncing projects across devices, plus an Edits assistant that analyses your Instagram insights and brainstorms ideas.
  • More agent skills for small businesses and creators, covering end-to-end marketing, business operations, content creation and optimization.
  • WhatsApp Plus tests — chat and media backup storage, and Focus Schedules for muting or hiding chats on a schedule.

The agent roadmap is the one to watch. If Meta Business Agent gets good enough to handle end-to-end marketing operations, the subscription stops being a visibility play and starts competing with tooling you already pay for.

§ Where to start

Where to start

  1. This week

    Open the subscription flow on each client account and screenshot the real price and feature set. "Starting at" plus regional variation means the published numbers are a floor, not a quote.

  2. This week

    Price out what a follow, a click and a resolved account flag are actually worth to the brand. Compare that against $49.99 per platform — the only tier worth modelling.

  3. Within 2 weeks

    If you subscribe, turn on exportable analytics first and baseline your organic reach before the carousel and search placement kick in. You cannot measure the lift without a clean before.

  4. Within 2 weeks

    Set a link-credit policy with the content team. Decide which posts get a credit before the month starts — credits don't carry back and don't come back.

  5. Within 30 days

    Decide whether it earns its keep against the media budget. Cancel if the organic lift is inside the noise band of normal week-to-week variance.

§ Takeaway

The one-line takeaway

Meta didn't just ship 50 features — it moved visibility, growth nudges, exportable data and real support behind a paywall, and priced the part that matters at $49.99 per platform. Whether Meta One is worth it depends on what those specific levers are worth to your account, not on FOMO — and not on the $14.99 headline.
