Framework Recommendations

Service-by-service implications of 'Performance partner for 8-figure operators'

Service Architecture Audit

If "performance partner for 8-figure operators" is true, this is what changes.

Reviewing the 2026 Ad Pros service deck against the positioning. The services are right. The architecture, pricing weight, and naming need surgery. Each tab below covers one service through the framework lens — what stays, what moves, what the position demands.

The deck is built for vendors. The position is partner.

The 2026 deck reads like a service menu — Creative Strategy, Creative Production, CPN, Media Buying, three products coming soon. That's what a competent agency would build. The problem is it's also what every competent agency has built. None of it is wrong. None of it is unique to "performance partner for 8-figure operators" either.

Three structural issues surface immediately when read through the framework lens.

Issue 1: The fee architecture says vendor

In the sample proposal, total fees are $24,060/month against $284K monthly spend — about 8.5% of spend. Of that, only $8,860 (37%) is variable. The other 63% is fixed. A real performance partner would invert that ratio: fixed should cover floor cost, variable should be the dominant component. Right now the kicker is the garnish, not the entrée.

Issue 2: The service language is feature-led, not outcome-led

"Creative Strategy," "Creative Production," "Media Buying" are the same three nouns 200 other agencies use. None of them name a partnership outcome. None of them filter for 8-figure operators. The deck would read identically if the target client were a $500K/year shop.

Issue 3: The "coming soon" trio is buried

AI Ads Library, Intel, and Agentic Workflows are the actual category-claim assets — proprietary AI infrastructure most agencies don't have. They're listed last and labeled "coming soon," which signals "side project" rather than "the thing that makes us different." Pirates' Languaging Law: the words you use shape the category. "Coming soon" is the wrong frame for the strongest assets.

The honest read The deck wins deals against weaker agencies. It does not yet win deals against better-positioned agencies. The fix is not to add more services. The fix is to restructure how the existing services are priced, named, and sequenced so they live up to the position.

Creative Strategy: rename it. The deliverable is the asset.

Tiers are $5K / $10K / $20K monthly. Deliverables include 22 named items — brand audit, 9 creative dimensions, 90-day roadmap, monthly memo, briefs, scorecards, QBRs.

Through the framework lens

Dunford on differentiated capabilities: the 9 Dimensions framework, the Creative Matrix, the Breakthrough classification (Loser / Winner / Breakthrough at 5% / 10%) are real differentiated capabilities. Most agencies don't have a named scoring system. This is the most defensible piece of the entire offering. But it's listed as deliverable #4 of 22, not as the hero.

Pirates' Languaging: "Creative Strategy" is generic noun-pair. Every agency claims it. The actual differentiated thing is the system — call it that. "The Breakthrough System." "The 9 Dimensions Engine." "Creative Performance Operations." Whatever the name, it should be the same name across the deck, the website, the IG bio, and Slack.

Play Bigger's POV: the underlying point of view is "creative is the largest unmanaged variable in performance, and most agencies are flying blind because they don't have a scoring system." That POV is implicit in the deck but never stated. Stating it once would do more work than 22 deliverable bullets.

Creative Production: this is the commodity tier.

Tiers are $4K / $8K / $16K monthly for 20 / 40 / 80 concepts. UGC recruiting, post-production, ad spy tools, AI UGC, copywriting, DAM.

Through the framework lens

Dunford on competitive alternatives: the real alternative isn't another agency's production team. It's the in-house creative team the operator already has, plus a freelance UGC roster, plus their existing post-production vendor. Those alternatives are cheaper. Production at this tier is hard to differentiate on — the work is largely commoditized, and AI is compressing margins fast.

Pirates' Magic Triangle: production is product-design work. It needs to fit the category. The category is "performance partner" — meaning every concept produced should be tagged to a hypothesis the strategy tier defined and a result the media buying tier will measure. Production in isolation is a vendor service. Production wired into a closed loop is a partner service.

Play Bigger's POV-product fit: production volume alone (80 concepts/month at the top tier) is a quantity claim. A category-king claim would be production volume tied to breakthrough yield — "we ship 80 concepts/month, we expect 4 breakthroughs minimum, and our Creative Matrix shows you exactly which dimensions we're testing."

The honest tension

Production is the service most exposed to AI commoditization in the next 24 months. The strategic move is not to make production look more premium. It's to position production as the execution arm of the Breakthrough System — so the operator buys the system and production is the throughput of it. If production stands alone, the price ceiling is set by what AI tools cost.

Recommendation

Production tier

Stop selling production as a standalone tier. Bundle it with Creative Strategy. The new top-of-deck offer is "The Breakthrough System: strategy + production + breakthrough guarantee." Production volume becomes a delivery parameter, not a pricing axis. Operators stop comparing $4K-$16K production tiers against freelance rosters and start buying the system.

Framework call

Pirates' Magic Triangle: product-category fit means the product (production) must serve the category (performance partnership). Standalone production breaks the triangle because it's purchasable as a vendor service. Bundled production reinforces it.

Creator Performance Network: this is the category claim.

Listed in the deck as "Coming Soon." The 8 deliverables include high-level strategy, creator video production, post-production, UGC management, recruiting, compliance, AI-generated content, and multi-channel distribution.

Through the framework lens

Pirates' Different Law: CPN is the only service in the deck that no major competitor offers in the same form. The 7/3 split (Ad Pros keeps 7% of spend on creator content, 3% passes to creators) is structural, not tactical. It changes what creators get paid for (performance, not deliverables) and what brands buy (creator economics, not creator content). This is genuinely different — not better.

Pirates' Languaging: "Creator Performance Network" is already a strong name. The acronym CPN is bookable. "Creator Performance Network" passes the Frame It / Name It / Claim It test — it frames the problem (creator content is performance-illegible), names the solution (a network with shared economics), and claims the space (no incumbent owns this term).

Play Bigger's Lightning Strike: CPN is the asset that justifies a Lightning Strike. It's structurally different, defensibly named, and tied to the partnership identity. The fact that it's listed as "Coming Soon" is a category-design crime — it should be the most prominent piece of the deck, not the most hidden.

Media Buying: the kicker is right. The base is too high.

Primary channels (Meta, Google, YouTube): $5K base + tiered variable from 4% down to 1.5% across $125K-$5M spend. Secondary channels (Applovin, TikTok, Native, etc.): $2.5K base + same tier structure starting at $62.5K.

Through the framework lens

Dunford on differentiated value: the variable kicker is the differentiated value mechanism. Operators understand "we eat what we kill" intuitively. Most agencies bill flat retainers regardless of outcome. The kicker structure is what makes "performance partner" credible — and it's already in place.

Pirates' Magic Triangle (company-category fit): the company has to live the category. If "performance partner" is the position, the company's revenue must come predominantly from performance. Right now the base retainers ($5K + $2.5K = $7.5K minimum across channels) are still meaningful even when variable is small. At $284K monthly spend (the proposal example), the variable is $8,860 — only 1.18x the base. The variable isn't dominant yet.

Play Bigger's POV fit: the POV is "agencies should be paid like operators, not like vendors." Operators don't get a $5K guarantee. Their comp is tied to performance. The base retainer is fine as a floor — but it should be smaller, and the variable should clearly dominate at the spend tiers Ad Pros targets.

Recommendation

Pricing structure

Don't change the kicker structure — it's right. Change the entry point. Raise the minimum spend for a Media Buying engagement from $125K/mo to $250K/mo (primary channels) and $125K/mo (secondary). At those floors, variable share is consistently above 60% and the partnership identity holds. Anything below that is vendor work and should either be priced as a one-time engagement or routed to a "growth" tier that explicitly isn't partnership-priced.

Framework call

Dunford's "best-fit customer" demands you know who you're not for. Operators below $250K/mo on primary channels are the wrong fit for the partnership pitch — not because they're bad clients but because the math doesn't make Ad Pros feel like a partner at that scale. Raise the floor. Let smaller brands self-select out.

The Dream Client filter is sharper than "8-figure operator."

The deck's Dream Client slide: $3M+/mo revenue ($250K minimum), $1M+/mo spend ($100K minimum), AOV $50-150, mass-market offer with clear avatar, proven scaling success, 30%+ revenue from repeat customers.

Through the framework lens

Dunford on best-fit customers: this is the best-articulated piece of the entire deck. Six concrete filters. Self-selecting. A $1M/year shop reading this knows it's not for them. A $50M shop reading this feels seen. This is what best-fit customer narrowness looks like done right.

Yoon's Superconsumers: the 30%+ repeat revenue criterion is doing more work than it gets credit for. It filters for brands with retention economics — the only kind of DTC business where performance media compounds. Brands with sub-30% repeat are acquiring forever; brands above 30% have an LTV story that justifies premium fees. This single filter is worth the rest combined.

Pirates' "Knowing who you're not for": the slide implicitly excludes B2B SaaS, low-AOV ecommerce ($<$50), single-purchase products with no repeat (e.g., mattresses), and any brand without proven scale. This is a feature, not a bug.

The disconnect

The Dream Client slide says one thing. The Media Buying entry tier ($125K/mo primary, $62.5K/mo secondary) says another. The Dream Client requires $1M+/mo spend. The Media Buying tier accepts $125K/mo. That's an 8x gap between stated audience and pricing audience. It signals that Ad Pros says "8-figure operator" but services anyone with a credit card.

Recommendation

Filter alignment

Promote the Dream Client filter into the entry criteria for every service. Either you meet the Dream Client filter and get the partnership pricing, or you don't and you get a different (smaller, more transactional) engagement. Don't run two parallel pricing schemes that contradict the Dream Client slide. The filter is the product.

Framework call

Dunford's recursive framework: the best-fit customer is the customer who values the differentiated value. If the differentiated value is partnership economics, sub-Dream-Client brands don't value it enough to pay the premium — and selling to them dilutes the position with everyone else.

Pirates' Magic Triangle (product-position fit): the product (the proposal) doesn't fit the position (performance partner). To fit, variable should dominate. At minimum 60% variable, ideally 70-80%.

Pricing redesign

Move toward Option A as the standard partnership pricing. Reserve Option B for the top-tier Dream Clients ($2M+/mo spend) where the math works overwhelmingly in Ad Pros' favor on success. Framework call

This is where Dunford and Pirates converge. Differentiated value (Dunford) and product-position fit (Pirates) both demand the variable side dominate. Without that change, "performance partner" is rhetoric the CFO can audit and find lacking.

Category Pirates

Thirty days, six moves.

Don't try to redesign the entire offering at once. The frameworks agree on what to do first, and the order matters.

Week 1 — Run Dunford end-to-end

Half-day

Block a half-day. Walk the 5-component sequence for Ad Pros. Output one page: competitive alternatives (in-house, freelance, generic agencies, fractional CMO), differentiated capabilities (kicker pricing, CPN, Intelligence Stack, senior operators), differentiated value (lower variance, higher ROAS, faster scale), best-fit (Dream Client filter), market category (performance partner). Treat this as the source of truth.

Week 1 — Languaging audit and replace

2-3 hours

Run the table from the Languaging tab against every public artifact. Replace in one sitting. Do not stretch this into a two-month rollout — saturation is the point. Update the website hero, IG bios, LinkedIn company tagline, email signatures, deck cover, contract preambles. Same week.

Week 2 — Map current Superconsumers

3-4 hours

Pull the last 12 months of clients. Score each on tenure × monthly spend × NPS × referrals generated. Identify the top 10%. Document what they have in common — vertical, AOV, repeat rate, founder type, prior agency history. That commonality is the new outbound ICP. Stop chasing anyone who doesn't match it.

Week 2 — Restructure the proposal

1 day

Build Option A pricing (floor + kicker, ~70% variable) as the default partnership proposal. Build Option B (outcome-based, ~85% variable) for top-tier accounts. Keep current structure as the explicit "vendor tier" for non-partnership engagements. Three pricing modes, named clearly. Update the sample proposal in the deck.

Week 3 — Lead with CPN

1 day deck rebuild

Move CPN from "coming soon" line item to the lead service in the deck. Drop the "Coming Soon" tag. Build out the 7/3 economics on the slide. Frame as "the only creator program where the agency, brand, and creator share performance upside." If CPN isn't fully operational yet, position the launch as a signed-up cohort.

Weeks 3-4 — Plan the Q3 Lightning Strike

Planning + drafting

Pick the date. Bundle the three "coming soon" assets into "The Ad Pros Intelligence Stack." Draft the manifesto (2,000-4,000 words on docs.adpros.com). Line up 3-5 podcast appearances. Build a public demo with anonymized client data. Confirm 2-3 named-client testimonials. Set the launch window for a single 2-3 week window in Q3 — not a slow drip.

The position is correct. The architecture supporting the position is not yet. Thirty days is enough to change the pricing weight, the languaging, the deck order, and the launch plan. Then nine months of steady drip until the new position becomes reputation.
