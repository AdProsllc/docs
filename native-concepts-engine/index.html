01 WHY THIS MATTERS

From days of production to one guided run

The Primitive Labs native concepts deck — 8 angle groups, 45+ headlines, 20 mocked-up Taboola ads — took a full manual cycle: research, angle strategy, copywriting, compliance checking, image generation, deck design. The Engine automates the production and keeps humans where they create the most value: judgment calls at four gates. Compliance is enforced before anything is generated, so we stop paying for copy and images that Taboola would reject.

The compounding effectEvery concept carries a permanent ID and a status lifecycle. When performance data flows back in later phases, wins attribute to specific angles and image patterns — so every batch makes the next one smarter. This is a system, not a template.

Step 1 of 5

Ingest — know the brand before writing a word

Minimum human input, maximum machine research. The operator fills a short onboarding form; AI agents research everything else.

The human provides

Brand name, the offer, landing page URL, banned topics, and how aggressive we're allowed to be with claims. Optional: reference docs and attachments. Five minutes, once per client.

The system researches

Customer personas, competitor ad angles, and category conventions — pulled from Pulse's competitive intelligence agents and web research. Gaps get filled automatically, not left blank.

1.1 THE CLAIMS INVENTORY

Every claim on the landing page, risk-rated before generation

The system reads the client's landing page and tags every claim as provable, risky, or banned. This becomes a hard rule set injected into all copy and image generation. Result: we stop producing ads Taboola will reject — the single biggest source of wasted production in native.

Example from the reference client"14 million Texans lost water in 2021" — provable, use freely. "90% of Americans won't survive" — risky, requires reviewer opt-in. "Removes 99.9999999% of contaminants" — banned, never generated.
GATE 1

Profile approval. A strategist reviews the assembled brand profile and claim ratings in a one-screen checklist. Text-only, fast — and nothing generates until it's signed off. Risky claims require an explicit opt-in here.

Step 2 of 5

Strategy — angles before assets

An angle is the psychological doorway an ad walks through. The Engine generates ten of them per run — each with a hypothesis, a target persona, and a risk rating — before a single headline or image exists.

Six of the ten angle types, illustrated with reference-client examples. Also in the taxonomy: question hooks, insider/expert, state-specific, and mechanism/product-forward.

GATE 2

Angle review — the money gate. The reviewer keeps, kills, or redirects each angle (minimum 5 survive). Killing a weak angle here costs nothing; discovering it after generating 20 images costs real credits and hours. This gate is why the Engine is cheap to run.

Step 3 of 5

Copy — 100 headlines, every one pre-checked

Claude writes ten headlines per surviving angle, grounded in two sources: our direct-response playbook and a library of proven winning ads tagged by angle type.

Grounded, not improvised

Every headline draws on the DR mindset doc (open loops, specificity, front-loaded messages) plus real winning examples from the corpus — so batch quality doesn't depend on who's prompting.

Auto-lint before any human sees it

A deterministic checker enforces the 60-character Taboola limit, casing and punctuation rules, and the client's banned-claims list. Failures regenerate silently. Reviewers never waste time on mechanical errors.

3.1 COMPLIANCE BUILT IN

Taboola's editorial rules — 60-character max (34–45 recommended), no all-caps, proper grammar, titles that truthfully reflect the landing page — are enforced as code, not as a reviewer's memory. Target: under 5% editorial disapproval on launched concepts.

GATE 3

Headline curation. The reviewer stars the best 6–10 headlines per angle in a one-click screen. Only starred headlines earn images — the second cost control.

Step 4 of 5

Images — native-feed realism at scale

Four images per approved headline, generated through Higgsfield to proven direct-response patterns — the photojournalistic, editorial look that makes native ads feel like content, not ads.

Pattern-driven, freshly composed

Each angle type has a visual pattern (fear → environmental desolation; authority → institutional settings; family → candlelit domestic scenes). Claude composes a fresh prompt per headline from the pattern rules, best practices, and sample references — consistency from the pattern, freshness from the composition.

The rules that make native work

Visualize the headline's noun — the dry pump, the empty shelf, the hearing room. Never the product. No text overlays, no logos, editorial realism only. The image opens the loop; the click closes it.

Self-improving libraryImages approved at Gate 4 get promoted into the pattern sample library. The Engine's visual quality compounds run over run — rejection rates are designed to fall with every batch.
GATE 4

Image review. A visual grid — approve, reject, or regenerate each image with one click, and confirm the headline–image pairings. Bad renders never reach a client.

Step 5 of 5

Delivery — the deck assembles itself

Approved concepts flow into the Ad Pros branded deck automatically — cover, native-ad mockups in Taboola feed frames, and a headline appendix grouped by angle. The same format as the Primitive Labs deck, produced in minutes instead of hours.

Client-ready package

Minimum 5 angles, 6–10 headlines each, 50–100 images. Account lead presents; the client relationship stays human.

Everything tracked

Every concept carries a permanent ID and status. When it launches and performs, results attribute back to the exact angle and pattern.

Feedback loops back

Client feedback is written into the brand profile, so run #2 starts smarter than run #1 — automatically.

5.1 WHAT COMES NEXT (POST-V1)

Phase 5 — Launch: approved concepts pushed straight into Taboola with tracking baked in. Phase 6 — Learn: performance data flows into BigQuery daily, winners auto-promote into the copy and image libraries, and fatigue triggers open the next batch automatically. The data model ships Phase 6-ready from day one.
