    Meta Creator Whitelisting SOP

    Partnership Ads access process, including dark/unpublished ads

    Overview

    What "Whitelisting" Actually Means on Meta

    Meta no longer uses the old "Facebook whitelisting" (Business Manager Ad Account access) — that's deprecated. The current mechanism is Partnership Ads (formerly Branded Content Ads), which lets a brand run ads using a creator's Instagram handle/identity without needing the creator's login credentials at all.

    There are two paths:

    1

    Partnership Ads (current standard)

    Creator authorizes via an in-app request from their Instagram account; no password/2FA sharing needed.

    2

    Legacy Business Manager access

    Only used for edge cases (e.g. creator wants brand to post directly on their behalf) — higher risk, avoid unless required.

    Step-by-step

    Core SOP

    Step 1 — Confirm Need & Notify Creator

    • Determine which ad(s)/creator(s) need whitelisting (usually top-performing organic or UGC content going into paid).
    • Message creator (via manager or Slack/email) explaining: "We'd like to boost your content as an ad through our brand's ad account. This requires a quick one-time authorization in Instagram — no passwords needed."

    Step 2 — Information to Collect From the Creator

    Info NeededWhy
    Instagram handle (exact @username)Used to send the Partnership Ads request
    Confirmation the IG account is a Creator or Business accountPersonal accounts can't authorize partnership ads
    Confirmation they have admin access to their own IG accountThey must accept the request themselves
    Email or phone tied to the account (optional)Only if troubleshooting is needed
    Not needed under the current system: passwords, 2FA codes, Facebook Page access, or Business Manager IDs from the creator.

    Step 3 — Brand Sends Partnership Ads Request

    • In Meta Ads Manager (or Business Suite), go to Brand Collabs / Partnership Ads tool under the client's ad account.
    • Enter the creator's Instagram handle.
    • Send the partnership request — this generates an in-app notification to the creator. This is an account-level request, not tied to any specific post.

    Step 4 — Creator Accepts (One-Time)

    • Creator opens Instagram → Settings → Business/Creator Tools → Branded Content / Partnership Ads → approves the pending request.
    • This single approval covers all future ads the brand builds under that creator's identity — published or dark — until revoked or expired.

    Step 5 — Confirm & Document

    • Verify status shows "Active" in the brand's Partnership Ads dashboard.
    • Log in tracker: creator handle, client, date approved, expiration (if any), which content/campaign it's tied to.

    Step 6 — Launch

    Once active, the ad can be built referencing the creator's handle as the "identity" — content shows as posted by the creator but runs through the brand's ad account/budget.

    Dark / Unpublished Ads

    Access Process When the Post Is Not Published on a Feed

    The authorization step is identical whether the ad will be published or dark — the difference is only in what happens after access is granted, and at ad-build time.

    1. Creator Account Eligibility Check

    • Instagram account must be a Business or Creator account (not personal).
    • Account must be in good standing with Meta's Branded Content Policies.
    • If the account doesn't meet requirements, they can't grant Partnership Ad permissions — and losing branded content tool access also revokes existing whitelisting.

    2. Brand Sends the Partnership Request

    Same account-level request as the core SOP — not tied to any specific post.

    3. Creator Approves (One-Time)

    One approval covers all future ads — published or dark — under that creator's identity.

    4. Brand Builds the Ad — Choose "Create New" (not "Use existing post")

    This is the actual fork in the process:

    Published postUnpublished / dark ad
    Ad Manager selection"Use existing post""Create new"
    Appears on creator's feed?Yes, with Paid Partnership tagNo — never public
    Creator sees in their analyticsFull post + ad metricsImpressions/reach only
    Best forSingle hero pieces, social proofHigh-volume iterative testing

    5. Ad Goes Live (Paid Only)

    • Ad runs showing the creator's name/handle/photo, funded and targeted entirely by the brand.
    • Never appears on the creator's profile, feed, or is visible to their followers organically.
    • Creator sees only impressions/reach in their own analytics — full conversion data stays in the brand's Ads Manager.

    Information Needed From the Creator (No Extra Steps for Dark Ads)

    InfoPurpose
    Exact Instagram handleTo send the partnership request
    Confirmation of Business/Creator account statusPersonal accounts can't authorize
    Their own in-app approval actionOnly they can accept — no credentials needed
    Key point There's no separate "dark post access" grant — one approval unlocks both dark and published formats. The distinction only shows up at ad-build time (Step 4) and in the written agreement, which should explicitly state whether whitelisting covers unpublished/dark ads, published boosts, or both.
    Notes & Edge Cases

    Additional Considerations

    • Access is revocable anytime by the creator — build in a check before major campaign launches to confirm it's still active.
    • Multiple brands can be authorized by one creator simultaneously — no conflict.
    • If a creator's account is personal (not Business/Creator), have them switch account type first — takes seconds in-app.
    • For UGC purchased outright with full usage rights but no live IG tag-in needed, Partnership Ads aren't required at all — just run it as a normal ad.
    • Document the agreement in writing: whitelisting window (start/end dates), scope of usage (Meta only vs. cross-platform), and termination clause. Most disputes happen because the agreement was verbal.
    Compliance note Some sources push the idea that Partnership Ads are mandatory for all creator-boosted content, framing non-compliant UGC ads as a "Deceptive Practice" violation. This traces to a single undated, uncited source — not a located Meta policy. Treat "always use Partnership Ads" as a best-practice / risk-mitigation stance rather than a confirmed hard Meta rule when advising clients.
