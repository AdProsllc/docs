    Layer 2

    Persona Profiles

    Six customer types built directly from the VOC above — not invented. Each includes who they are, what's holding them back, what finally converts them, and the real words they used to describe the experience.

    1
    The Veteran Nurse With a Condition
    Katie G. • Clemonce M. • Kelly Craft • Video Lady • Catherine I. • Kathy L.R.
    Who they are
    Experienced nurse (8–40+ years) managing a specific physical condition — knee replacement, plantar fasciitis, back pain, neuropathy, or dialysis-unit hazards. They work 12–16 hour shifts and their body pays a price every day.
    What they're feeling before they buy
    Tired of trying things that don't work. They've spent years and money on shoes that promised relief and delivered nothing. They're desperate but guarded — burned too many times to get excited easily.
    What's stopping them
    Skepticism from repeated disappointment. They've tried "everything." Another pair of shoes feels like another risk.
    What finally converts them
    Social proof from someone who also has their condition. A guarantee that removes the risk. Or just wearing one pair — the immediate feeling makes it undeniable.
    Their real words
    "I've never ever bought a pair that stops foot pain immediately — these did."
    "God Send. You feel like you're walking on clouds, and your feet don't hurt after a double shift."
    "lifesaver… 16 pairs and counting."
    2
    The Skeptic / Reluctant Convert
    Tracy C. • Molliny V.
    Who they are
    A nurse who saw the ad — maybe multiple times — and resisted. They don't trust targeted ads. They buy shoes from brands they already know. Breaking this pattern requires accumulated social pressure.
    What they're feeling before they buy
    Suspicious. "This is probably just another overhyped shoe brand." They've seen too many DTC ads promise and under-deliver. But the shoes keep showing up. The reviews pile up. Eventually curiosity wins.
    What's stopping them
    Distrust of the ad channel itself ("pop up adds"), brand loyalty to existing options, fear of wasting money.
    What finally converts them
    Volume of social proof. A moment of weakness (worn out pair, upcoming event). A risk-removing offer (free returns, trial period). Then the product itself seals it — the first wear has to deliver immediately.
    Their real words
    "I don't always trust pop up adds but these were in my rotation for weeks and I finally caved, best decision EVER."
    "I was unsure at first… glad that they live up to the reviews."
    "The moment I put them on I knew right away these were going to be my new fav shoe."
    3
    The Loyalist / Multi-Pair Collector
    Katie G. (16 pairs) • Sue Encinias • Kelly Craft (7 pairs) • Maria G. (9 pairs) • Kathy L.R.
    Who they are
    A past buyer who's already converted — now building a collection. They reorder different colors and styles, stack B2G1 deals, and often influence their unit. They're also the most powerful organic social proof engine NB has.
    What they're feeling before they buy (again)
    Zero doubt. They're hunting for a deal or a new colorway. The only "decision" is which style to get next. Their attention is on value and variety.
    What's stopping them (if anything)
    Price at full retail. If a deal or bundle is available, nothing stops them.
    What converts them (again)
    New colorway drops. Bundle deals (B2G1, multi-pack). Loyalty perks. Product expansion (new styles, seasonal releases).
    Their real words
    "16 pairs and counting."
    "Second order — buy 2 get 1 free was the best deal! I'm ready to order again."
    "Once you try one pair you can't stop. My whole unit wears them now."
    "I plan to order more colors and styles."
    4
    The Gift Buyer
    Jessica Johnson • Anonymous (graduation gift)
    Who they are
    Often not a nurse themselves. They're buying for someone they love — a niece with diabetes, a daughter starting her first nursing job, a spouse with foot problems. The purchase is an act of care.
    What they're feeling before they buy
    They want to give something that actually helps, not just a gift card. They feel responsible for getting it right. Reviews from people in similar situations (diabetic foot care, new-nurse prep) are critical proof points.
    What's stopping them
    Uncertainty about fit (for someone else's feet). Size anxiety. Fear of buying something the recipient already has or doesn't like.
    What converts them
    Reviews specifically mentioning gifting or receiving as a gift. A free returns / easy exchange policy. Seasonal gifting angles (Nurses Week, graduation, Christmas, birthdays).
    Their real words
    "I buy these every year for my niece and she is diabetic — they help her a lot."
    "She says there's no other shoe she'll wear."
    "her feet felt nothing like she expected after 12 hours."
    5
    The Closed-Shoe Avoider
    Molliny V.
    Who they are
    Has wide, flat, or sensitive feet and has learned the hard way that most closed-toe shoes become unbearable after a few hours. They've defaulted to clogs, slip-ons, or open-toe options — even if they're not ideal for the hospital floor.
    What they're feeling before they buy
    "This is probably going to be like every other closed shoe — looks fine for the first hour, nightmare by hour four." Deep-seated conditioning against closed footwear.
    What's stopping them
    Prior experience with closed shoes that compressed wide feet. No trust that any enclosed shoe can actually be comfortable for a full shift.
    What converts them
    A review from someone with the SAME foot type (wide, flat) who wore them all day. Molliny's own quote is the best ad for this segment. Wide-width callout in product copy.
    Their real words (Molliny is the persona)
    "I normally dislike closed shoes, but I can spend the whole working day in these."
    "Very comfortable shoes for my wide flat feet."
    6
    The Cross-Over Non-Nurse
    Victoria (flight attendant) • Anonymous (FMLA / injury recovery)
    Who they are
    On their feet all day — but not in healthcare. Flight attendants, retail workers, teachers, hospitality staff, people recovering from back injuries or surgeries. They found Nurse Babes through the algorithm, not brand intent.
    What they're feeling before they buy
    "Is this actually for me? The name says 'Nurse Babes' but I'm not a nurse." There's a slight identity friction — but once they see the reviews and results, it overrides the brand-name hesitation.
    What's stopping them
    Identity mismatch — "I'm not a nurse." Also physical therapist or doctor hasn't specifically recommended this brand.
    What converts them
    Someone like them in the reviews. Victoria's "I am not a nurse but..." framing is the conversion message itself. Copy that explicitly invites non-nurses ("Anyone on their feet all day").
    Their real words
    "I am not a nurse but I found Nurse Babes shoes scrolling. I wear them on every flight now. Walking on air."
    "I actually walk faster and with better posture — my back pain is basically gone."
    "game changer for my mobility… I can walk to the kitchen without dreading it."
    Layer 3

    Copywriting Cheat Sheets

    One cheat sheet per persona. Proven hooks, VOC phrases to lift verbatim (marked in teal), language to never use in paid ads, and the emotional arc your creative should follow.

    ⚠️ Compliance Lock (Adrienne Directive)NEVER use in paid ads: pain-free / pain-relief / cure / treat / heal / fix / medical-grade / therapeutic. Safe alternatives: comfort / support / all-day / cloud-like / show up strong. Customer quote testimonials are EXEMPT (third-party voice). FTC Gravity Defyer precedent Feb 2025 ($175K penalty) — current NB copy may be at risk.

    The Veteran Nurse With a Condition
    Targeting: 8–40 yr nurses with plantar fasciitis, back pain, knee issues, neuropathy, dialysis units
    🪝 Hooks That Work
    "18 years as an ER nurse. Left knee replacement. 16-hour shifts. These are a lifesaver."
    "After 23 years with plantar fasciitis, I finally found a God Send."
    "I've tried EVERYTHING for my back pain. Pair #7 just arrived."
    "40+ years on your feet. You've earned a shoe that actually works."
    "If you've had a knee replacement, you need to see this."
    💬 Lift These Verbatim
    "lifesaver"
    "God Send"
    "walking on clouds, and your feet don't hurt after a double shift"
    "I've never ever bought a pair that stops foot pain immediately"
    "My feet feel like they're floating"
    "feel like a miracle"
    "My back doesn't hurt nearly as much"
    ✅ These are customer quotes — FTC-exempt for paid ads
    🚫 Never Say / Emotional Arc
    pain-free
    therapeutic
    treats your condition
    medical-grade
    🧠 Emotional Arc
    →Validation: Name their condition. Show you understand the specific pain of a double shift with plantar fasciitis.
    →Exhaustion: Acknowledge they've tried everything. Don't oversell — earn trust first.
    →Proof: Social proof from someone with the same condition. Specific numbers (16 pairs, 23 years).
    →Relief: Let THEM describe the feeling. Use their words (lifesaver, God Send, clouds).
